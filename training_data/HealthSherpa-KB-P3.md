## Article 7: Enrolling a Family with Mixed Immigration Status

**Meta Description:** Guide for agents enrolling ACA marketplace applications for families with mixed citizenship and immigration status. Learn eligibility rules and application strategies.

**Search Terms:**
- Mixed immigration status family ACA enrollment
- Non-citizen spouse health insurance marketplace
- DACA recipients health insurance eligibility
- Household members different immigration status
- Lawfully present immigrants ACA coverage

### Problem Statement
You're assisting a family where household members have different immigration statuses (e.g., U.S. citizen children, lawfully present immigrant parent, undocumented parent). You need to understand who is eligible for Marketplace coverage, how to split applications, and how subsidies are calculated for mixed-status families.

### Immigration Status and ACA Eligibility Rules

**WHO IS ELIGIBLE for Marketplace Coverage:**

✅ **U.S. Citizens** (including those born in U.S. territories like Puerto Rico)
✅ **U.S. Nationals**
✅ **Lawfully Present Immigrants**, including:
- Lawful Permanent Residents (Green Card holders)
- Asylees and Refugees
- Those granted withholding of deportation/removal
- Cuban/Haitian entrants
- Parolees (duration of at least 1 year)
- Conditional entrants
- Battered spouse, child, or parent under VAWA
- Victims of trafficking (T-Visa)
- Victims of criminal activity (U-Visa)
- Special Immigrant Juvenile Status (SIJS)
- Registry applicants
- Temporary Protected Status (TPS)
- Deferred Enforced Departure (DED)
- Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA) - **NOT ELIGIBLE** (federal courts have ruled DACA recipients cannot enroll in Marketplace plans or receive subsidies)
- Employment Authorization Document (EAD) holders (if in lawfully present category)
- Students on F-1, J-1, M-1 visas (if meet other criteria)
- H-1B, H-2A, H-2B visa holders
- Tourist/visitor visa holders (B-1/B-2) - generally NOT eligible unless special circumstances

❌ **NOT ELIGIBLE for Marketplace Coverage:**
- Undocumented immigrants
- DACA recipients (as of current federal rulings)
- Those with pending asylum applications (generally not eligible until granted)
- Non-immigrant visa holders here temporarily for tourism

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Even though undocumented family members cannot enroll in Marketplace coverage, they MUST still be included on the application as household members for income and household size calculations.

### Mixed-Status Family Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: Citizen Children with Undocumented Parents

**Family Composition:**
- Parent A: Undocumented immigrant
- Parent B: Undocumented immigrant  
- Child 1 (age 8): U.S. citizen (born in U.S.)
- Child 2 (age 5): U.S. citizen (born in U.S.)

**Eligibility:**
- Children: ELIGIBLE for Marketplace (may also qualify for Medicaid/CHIP depending on state and income)
- Parents: NOT eligible for Marketplace

**Application Strategy:**

**Option A: Enroll Only Eligible Family Members**
1. Create ONE application on HealthSherpa
2. List entire household (all 4 people):
   - Parent A (not applying for coverage, immigration status: "Not lawfully present")
   - Parent B (not applying for coverage, immigration status: "Not lawfully present")
   - Child 1 (applying for coverage, U.S. citizen)
   - Child 2 (applying for coverage, U.S. citizen)
3. Report total household income (income from BOTH parents + any children's income)
4. Household size: 4 people
5. Only children will be evaluated for eligibility and enrolled

**Subsidy Calculation:**
- Based on full household income (all 4 people's income)
- Household size: 4 (all family members count)
- Only children receive subsidies (parents not eligible)

**Example Calculation:**
```
Household: 4 people
Total income: $50,000 (both parents' combined income)
FPL for household of 4: $31,200 (2025)
Income as % of FPL: $50,000 / $31,200 = 160% FPL
Subsidy: Yes, children qualify for premium tax credits

Monthly benchmark premium for 2 children (ages 5 & 8): $450
Expected contribution at 160% FPL: ~3.5% of income = $146/month
Monthly subsidy: $450 - $146 = $304/month
Family pays: $146/month for children's coverage
```

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Parents' income counts even though they're not applying. Do NOT split household or omit parents' income to artificially increase subsidy.

**Privacy Protections:**
- Information about undocumented family members is protected
- Cannot be shared with immigration enforcement (with limited exceptions for fraud investigation)
- Parents can safely include their information without immigration consequences
- Parents do NOT need to provide SSN (leave blank or indicate "not applying")

[SCREENSHOT: Application showing household with mixed status - checkbox for "not applying for coverage"]

**Option B: Check Medicaid/CHIP Eligibility for Children First**

Before enrolling in Marketplace:
1. Check if children qualify for Medicaid or CHIP (often income limits up to 200-300% FPL depending on state)
2. Tennessee: CoverKids (CHIP) covers children in families up to 211% FPL
3. If eligible for Medicaid/CHIP, enroll there instead (free or very low cost)
4. If over income limits, proceed with Marketplace application

**How to Check:**
- HealthSherpa application will screen for Medicaid/CHIP eligibility automatically
- If flagged as Medicaid-eligible, system will redirect to state Medicaid application
- OR visit https://www.healthcare.gov/medicaid-chip/getting-medicaid-chip/

#### Scenario 2: Citizen Parent with Lawfully Present Immigrant Spouse and Children

**Family Composition:**
- Parent A: U.S. citizen
- Parent B: Lawful Permanent Resident (Green Card)
- Child 1 (age 3): U.S. citizen (derived citizenship)
- Child 2 (age 1): U.S. citizen (born in U.S.)

**Eligibility:**
- All family members: ELIGIBLE for Marketplace
- May also be eligible for Medicaid/CHIP depending on immigration status waiting period

**5-Year Bar for Immigrants:**
Lawfully present immigrants may be subject to 5-year waiting period before qualifying for Medicaid (does NOT apply to Marketplace):
- Applies to most lawful permanent residents
- Does NOT apply to: refugees, asylees, Cuban/Haitian entrants, certain trafficking victims, veterans, active military
- Waiting period starts from date of obtaining qualified status
- Children may be exempt from 5-year bar in many states

**Application Strategy:**

**Single Application for All Members:**
1. Create ONE application
2. List all 4 household members applying for coverage
3. Provide immigration documentation for Parent B:
   - Permanent Resident Card number (I-551)
   - Alien Registration Number (A-Number)
   - Card expiration date
   - Date status obtained

**Documents Required for Lawful Permanent Resident:**
- Permanent Resident Card (Green Card) - front and back
- OR I-551 stamp in passport
- OR I-327 Reentry Permit
- Must upload within 95 days if data doesn't verify automatically

**Subsidy Calculation:**
- Full household income (both parents)
- Household size: 4
- All 4 members can receive subsidies

---

#### Scenario 3: U.S. Citizen with Non-Immigrant Visa Spouse (Temporary Status)

**Family Composition:**
- Parent A: U.S. citizen
- Parent B: H-1B visa holder (work visa)
- Child 1 (age 2): U.S. citizen

**Eligibility:**
- Parent A: ELIGIBLE (U.S. citizen)
- Parent B: MAY BE ELIGIBLE (depends on visa type and length of stay)
- Child 1: ELIGIBLE (U.S. citizen)

**H-1B Visa Eligibility:**
- Generally lawfully present for at least one year
- CAN apply for Marketplace coverage
- MUST provide visa documentation and I-94

**Application Strategy:**
1. Enroll all 3 household members
2. Provide Parent B's immigration documentation:
   - I-94 Arrival/Departure Record
   - Passport with H-1B visa stamp
   - I-797 Notice of Action (approval notice)

**Alternative Consideration:**
- H-1B workers often have employer-sponsored coverage
- If Parent B has employer plan, they're NOT eligible for Marketplace premium tax credits (if employer coverage is affordable)
- May still enroll but pay full price

---

#### Scenario 4: DACA Recipient in Family (Current Federal Rule)

**Family Composition:**
- Parent A: U.S. citizen
- Parent B: DACA (Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals) recipient
- Child 1: U.S. citizen

**Eligibility Under Current Law:**
- Parent A: ELIGIBLE
- Parent B: NOT ELIGIBLE for Marketplace (federal courts ruled DACA recipients cannot access Marketplace as of 2024-2025)
- Child 1: ELIGIBLE

**Application Strategy:**
1. List all 3 household members
2. Parent B: Select "Not applying for coverage" (include in household size and income)
3. Enroll Parent A and Child 1
4. Report full household income including Parent B's income
5. Household size: 3

**Alternative Coverage for DACA Parent:**
- Check for state-funded programs (some states offer coverage)
- Short-term health insurance (limited benefits, not ACA-compliant)
- Community health centers (sliding fee scale)
- Direct primary care memberships

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** DACA policy is subject to legal challenges and may change. Always check current eligibility rules at healthcare.gov before enrollment.

### Application Process for Mixed-Status Families

#### Step 1: Gather Documentation for ALL Household Members

**For U.S. Citizens:**
- Social Security Numbers
- Date of birth

**For Lawfully Present Immigrants:**
- I-94 number (Arrival/Departure Record)
- Passport number and country of issuance
- Visa number and type (if applicable)
- Alien Registration Number (A-Number)
- Certificate of Naturalization or Citizenship number
- Permanent Resident Card (Green Card) number
- Employment Authorization Document (EAD) number
- Other applicable immigration document numbers

**For Undocumented/Not Applying Members:**
- Date of birth (required)
- Social Security Number (optional - can leave blank if not applying)
- Income information (REQUIRED - must report their income)

#### Step 2: Create Application in HealthSherpa

**Navigate to Application Section:**
1. Start new consumer application
2. Enter primary applicant (usually U.S. citizen if applying)

**Add Household Members:**
1. Click "Add person"
2. For EACH person:
   - Name
   - Date of birth
   - SSN (if applying and have one)
   - Relationship
   - **Check or uncheck "Applying for coverage"** (CRITICAL)
   - Select immigration status from dropdown

**Immigration Status Dropdown Options:**
- U.S. Citizen
- U.S. National
- Lawfully Present Immigrant
- Not Lawfully Present Immigrant (for undocumented family members who are part of household but not applying)

[SCREENSHOT: Immigration status dropdown showing options]

#### Step 3: Complete Immigration Details for Lawfully Present Members

For each lawfully present immigrant APPLYING for coverage:

**Select Document Type:**
- Permanent Resident Card (I-551)
- Reentry Permit (I-327)
- Refugee Travel Document (I-571)
- Employment Authorization Document (I-766)
- Arrival/Departure Record (I-94/I-94A)
- Foreign Passport
- Certificate of Naturalization (N-550/N-570)
- Certificate of Citizenship (N-560/N-561)
- Others (full list in dropdown)

**Enter Document Information:**
- Document number (exactly as it appears)
- Issuing country (for passports)
- Alien Number (A-Number) if applicable
- Card expiration date
- SEVIS ID (for student visas)

**Provide Additional Details:**
- Date immigration status obtained
- Is status temporary or permanent?
- Expiration date (if temporary status)

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Enter numbers exactly as they appear on documents. Even one digit wrong will trigger Data Matching Issue (DMI).

[SCREENSHOT: Immigration document entry screen with sample I-551 number field]

#### Step 4: Report Complete Household Income

**Include Income From ALL Household Members**, even those not applying:

**Wage Income:**
- W-2 income from all working household members
- Wages from undocumented parents COUNT in household income
- Use last year's tax return as starting point, adjust for current year

**Self-Employment Income:**
- Report net income (gross receipts minus business expenses)
- Include cash income
- Include income from undocumented family members' self-employment

**Other Income:**
- Social Security (but NOT SSI)
- Unemployment benefits
- Pension/retirement
- Investment income
- Rental income
- Alimony (for divorces before 2019)

**Example Mixed-Status Household Income:**
```
Parent A (undocumented, not applying): $30,000 W-2 income
Parent B (undocumented, not applying): $15,000 self-employment
Child 1 (U.S. citizen, applying): $0
Child 2 (U.S. citizen, applying): $0

Total Household Income: $45,000 (must include all income)
```

#### Step 5: Determine Household Size

**Count ALL people in the tax household:**
- Yourself
- Your spouse (if married)
- Your dependents (children, others you claim)
- Include people NOT applying for coverage
- Include undocumented family members

**Tax Filing Status Matters:**
- If married filing jointly: Both spouses + dependents
- If married filing separately: Depends on living situation (complex - see below)
- If head of household: Yourself + dependents
- If single: Yourself + anyone you claim as dependent

**Special Household Rules:**

**Married Couples:**
- Generally counted as one tax household
- Both spouses' income counts
- Both counted in household size
- Exception: Married filing separately due to domestic abuse/abandonment

**Children:**
- If claimed as dependent: Part of parent's tax household
- If NOT claimed (over age 18, financially independent): Separate household
- Foster children: Can be included if you're supporting them

#### Step 6: Review Eligibility Results

After submitting application:

**Marketplace will return eligibility determination for each applying member:**
- **Eligible for Marketplace with APTC** (premium tax credits)
- **Eligible for Marketplace without APTC** (over 400% FPL or other reason)
- **Eligible for Medicaid or CHIP** (system redirects to state Medicaid)
- **Not Eligible** (immigration status, already has coverage, etc.)

**Members NOT applying for coverage:**
- Will NOT receive eligibility determination
- Listed as "Not applying"
- Still counted in household size and income calculations

[SCREENSHOT: Eligibility results showing mixed household - some members eligible, others not applying]

#### Step 7: Enroll Eligible Members in Plan

**Select Plan:**
- Shop plans based on eligible members only
- Premium shown is for eligible members
- Check provider networks include doctors for those enrolling

**Example:**
```
Family of 4, only 2 children eligible and enrolling
Plan premium for 2 children ages 5 & 8: $450/month
Subsidy: $304/month
Family pays: $146/month

(Parents not enrolled, so their ages don't affect premium)
```

**Make First Payment:**
- Pay for enrolled members only
- Set up auto-pay
- Coverage begins on effective date

#### Step 8: Upload Immigration Documents

**If Data Matching Issue (DMI) Triggered:**

Immigration status is one of the most common DMI types.

**Timeline:** 95 days to upload immigration documents (5 days longer than other DMIs)

**What to Upload:**
- Clear, legible copy of immigration document entered on application
- Front AND back of cards (Green Card, EAD)
- All pages of passport (photo page + visa stamp page)
- I-94 printout from CBP website (if entering document number didn't verify)

**File Requirements:**
- Accepted formats: PDF, JPG, PNG, TIFF
- Max file size: 10MB
- Must be readable (not blurry)

**Where to Upload:**
1. Log into HealthCare.gov
2. Click "Documents"
3. Select document type "Immigration document"
4. Choose specific document type from dropdown
5. Upload file

**OR Through HealthSherpa:**
1. Dashboard shows "Documents Due"
2. Click "Upload documents"
3. Select person and document type
4. Upload

**Processing Time:** 10-30 business days

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** If documents not submitted within 95 days, coverage for immigrant family members may be terminated retroactively.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake #1: Omitting Undocumented Family Members from Application**

❌ **Wrong Approach:**
- List only eligible family members
- Don't mention undocumented parents
- Try to increase subsidy by reducing household size

✅ **Correct Approach:**
- List ALL household members, including those not applying
- Check "Not applying for coverage" for ineligible members
- Include their income in household total
- Include them in household size

**Why It Matters:**
- Violates application rules (must attest truthfulness)
- Can result in subsidy repayment at tax time
- May be considered fraud
- When you file taxes, your actual household will be revealed

---

**Mistake #2: Not Including Undocumented Parents' Income**

❌ **Wrong Approach:**
- Only report eligible family members' income
- Think undocumented income "doesn't count"

✅ **Correct Approach:**
- Report ALL household income, including undocumented members
- Include W-2 income, self-employment, cash income
- Use tax return as guide (if filed)

**Why It Matters:**
- Subsidy is based on household MAGI
- Reconciliation happens on tax return
- Incorrect income = subsidy repayment or IRS penalty

---

**Mistake #3: Entering Immigration Document Numbers Incorrectly**

❌ **Common Errors:**
- Transposed digits
- Including dashes or spaces where they shouldn't be
- Wrong document type selected
- Expired documents

✅ **Correct Approach:**
- Enter numbers EXACTLY as they appear
- Double-check every digit
- Verify document is current (not expired)
- Have physical document in hand while entering

**Example of Correct Entry:**
```
Permanent Resident Card:
Card Number (I-551): ABC1234567890 (enter exactly, no spaces)
Alien Number: A123456789 (include the "A")
Expiration Date: 05/15/2032
Category: LPR (Lawful Permanent Resident)
```

---

**Mistake #4: Assuming Student Visa Holders Can't Get Coverage**

**Reality:**
- Some student visa holders (F-1, J-1, M-1) MAY be eligible if:
  - Here for at least 1 year
  - Meet other criteria
- Always check eligibility rather than assuming

**What to Do:**
- Enter visa information on application
- Let Marketplace determine eligibility
- Provide I-20 or DS-2019 form
- Provide I-94

---

**Mistake #5: Not Coordinating with Other Coverage**

**Check First:**
- Is anyone eligible for employer coverage?
- Is anyone eligible for Medicaid/CHIP?
- Does anyone have Medicare?

**Employer Coverage Rule:**
- If eligible for affordable employer coverage, NOT eligible for Marketplace subsidies
- Applies even in mixed-status household
- "Affordable" = employee-only premium ≤ 9.02% of household income (2024)

**What to Do:**
- Ask about ALL household members' other coverage options
- Employer coverage for one person doesn't disqualify others
- Each person evaluated individually

### Special Situations

#### Situation 1: Pregnant Immigrant Who Just Arrived

**Question:** Pregnant woman on tourist visa, about to give birth in U.S. Can baby get coverage?

**Answer:**
- Mother: Generally NOT eligible for Marketplace (tourist visa)
- May be eligible for emergency Medicaid for labor/delivery in some states
- Baby: Automatically U.S. citizen (if born in U.S.), ELIGIBLE for Marketplace or Medicaid
- Can enroll baby within 60 days of birth (Special Enrollment Period)
- Coverage is retroactive to birth date

**What to Do:**
1. After birth, obtain baby's birth certificate
2. Apply for baby's Social Security Number
3. Create Marketplace application listing:
   - Mother (not applying, tourist visa status)
   - Baby (applying, U.S. citizen)
4. Check Medicaid eligibility for baby first (likely eligible)
5. If over Medicaid limits, enroll in Marketplace

---

#### Situation 2: Spouse on H-1B Visa Lost Job

**Question:** H-1B visa holder was employed, had employer coverage. Lost job. Can they enroll in Marketplace?

**Answer:**
- Loss of job-based coverage = qualifying event for Special Enrollment Period
- H-1B status may still be valid (grace period typically 60 days to find new job or leave U.S.)
- Can enroll in Marketplace during 60-day grace period
- BUT: If H-1B status expires and becomes unlawful presence, coverage may be terminated

**What to Do:**
1. Verify H-1B status still valid
2. Check visa expiration and I-94 validity
3. Enroll within 60 days of losing employer coverage
4. Provide COBRA notice as proof of qualifying event
5. Monitor visa status - if expires, notify Marketplace

---

#### Situation 3: Green Card Holder Has Been in U.S. for 3 Years - Medicaid vs. Marketplace?

**Question:** Lawful Permanent Resident, in U.S. for 3 years (not yet past 5-year bar), income at 120% FPL. Medicaid or Marketplace?

**Answer:**
- Depends on state and individual circumstances
- If state has NOT expanded Medicaid: Likely Marketplace (with large subsidy)
- If state HAS expanded Medicaid: Depends on if state exempted immigrants from 5-year bar

**Tennessee (Medicaid NOT Expanded):**
- Adults generally don't qualify for Medicaid (except pregnant women, disabled, etc.)
- Marketplace is primary option
- At 120% FPL, subsidy is very high

**What to Do:**
1. Enter information on Marketplace application
2. System will auto-screen for Medicaid
3. If flagged for Medicaid, apply through state
4. If not Medicaid-eligible, enroll in Marketplace with subsidy

---

#### Situation 4: Family Members in Different States

**Question:** Married couple files taxes jointly. Spouse A in Tennessee, Spouse B in Texas (work assignment). Children in Tennessee. How to apply?

**Answer:**
- Each person applies in their state of residence
- Household size and income calculations still based on tax filing
- Premium tax credits split between applications
- More complex - may need tax professional help at year-end

**What to Do:**
1. Spouse A + children: Apply in Tennessee Marketplace
2. Spouse B: Apply in Texas Marketplace
3. Both applications list same household size (all family members)
4. Both report same total household income
5. Each application gets portion of subsidy
6. At tax reconciliation, combine Form 1095-As from both states

### Tax Implications for Mixed-Status Families

#### Filing Requirements

**If Married to Non-Citizen:**
- Can file Married Filing Jointly OR Married Filing Separately
- Jointly: May need Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) for spouse without SSN
- Separately: More complex, may lose subsidy eligibility

**Premium Tax Credit Reconciliation:**
- Must file Form 8962 if received advance premium tax credits
- Report actual household income
- Compare to projected income used during enrollment
- May owe repayment or receive additional credit

#### ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number)

**What is ITIN:**
- 9-digit number for people who need to file taxes but don't have SSN
- Format: 9XX-XX-XXXX (always starts with 9)
- Issued by IRS

**Who Needs ITIN:**
- Undocumented immigrants who file taxes
- Spouses of U.S. citizens filing jointly
- Dependents claimed on tax returns

**How to Get ITIN:**
- File Form W-7 with IRS
- Provide proof of identity and foreign status
- Typically takes 7-11 weeks to receive

**For Marketplace Purposes:**
- Can list ITIN on application for household members not applying for coverage
- Helps with verification of tax filing
- NOT a substitute for SSN for those applying for coverage

### Privacy and Immigration Enforcement Concerns

**Question:** Will immigration authorities get information about undocumented family members on my application?

**Answer:**
- Federal law PROHIBITS sharing Marketplace application information with immigration enforcement (with narrow exceptions)
- Information used ONLY for Marketplace eligibility and subsidies
- Applies to HealthCare.gov and HealthSherpa

**Exceptions:**
- Fraud investigation
- Law enforcement with proper warrant

**What This Means:**
- Safe for families to include undocumented members on application
- Won't trigger immigration action
- Parents can apply for eligible children without fear

**Protections Under Law:**
- Section 1411(g) of the Affordable Care Act
- Restricts use and disclosure of information
- Only shared with specific agencies for eligibility purposes

### Best Practices for Agents

**1. Build Trust with Mixed-Status Families**
- Explain privacy protections
- Assure them information won't be shared with immigration
- Be sensitive to concerns and fears
- Provide information in their preferred language

**2. Always Include Full Household**
- Even if not applying for coverage
- Explain why it's necessary (accurate subsidy, tax reconciliation)
- Never suggest omitting household members to increase subsidy

**3. Double-Check Immigration Document Numbers**
- Have client bring physical documents to appointment
- Enter numbers while looking at document
- Verify every digit
- Confirm expiration dates

**4. Set Expectations About DMIs**
- Explain that immigration DMIs are common
- Provide instructions for uploading documents
- Give 95-day deadline
- Offer to help with uploads

**5. Coordinate with Medicaid**
- Check children's Medicaid/CHIP eligibility first
- Medicaid is often better (lower or no cost)
- Refer to local Medicaid office if needed

**6. Document Everything**
- Keep copies of immigration documents you reviewed
- Note which documents were used on application
- Track DMI resolution status
- Follow up with clients

## Article 8: Managing Passive Renewals vs Active Re-enrollment

**Meta Description:** Learn about ACA plan crosswalks, passive renewal process, when to actively re-enroll clients, and commission implications for agents.

**Search Terms:**
- ACA passive renewal process
- Plan crosswalk HealthSherpa
- Automatic re-enrollment Marketplace
- When to re-enroll clients Open Enrollment
- Active vs passive renewal commissions

### Problem Statement
As an agent, you need to understand when clients are automatically renewed (passive renewal), when their plan is "crosswalked" to a different plan, and when you should actively help them re-enroll to ensure they get the best coverage and you receive commissions.

### Understanding Passive Renewal vs. Active Re-enrollment

**Passive Renewal (Automatic Re-enrollment):**
- Client does NOT return to Marketplace during Open Enrollment
- Automatically renewed into their current plan for the next year
- OR automatically "crosswalked" to a new plan if current plan discontinued
- Same subsidy recalculated based on updated income and FPL
- Coverage continues seamlessly with no gap

**Active Re-enrollment:**
- Client (with agent assistance) returns to Marketplace during Open Enrollment
- Actively selects a plan (same plan or different plan)
- Reviews and updates income, household, and other information
- Can shop for better rates or benefits
- Agent receives new commission

⚠️ **IMPORTANT FOR AGENTS:** Passive renewals may NOT generate new commissions. Active re-enrollments typically DO generate commissions. Check carrier-specific policies.

### The Plan Crosswalk Process

**What is a Crosswalk?**
When a health insurance plan is discontinued or an insurer leaves the Marketplace, affected enrollees are automatically transitioned ("crosswalked") to a similar plan to avoid coverage gaps.

**Who Controls Crosswalks:**
- **If carrier is staying in Marketplace but discontinuing specific plan:** Carrier decides the crosswalk plan (must be "most similar")
- **If carrier is leaving Marketplace entirely:** State or federal Marketplace decides the crosswalk plan (to a different carrier)

**Crosswalk Hierarchy (CMS Rules for "Most Similar Plan"):**

When carrier must crosswalk to new plan, they follow this priority:

1. **Same metal tier** (Bronze to Bronze, Silver to Silver, etc.)
2. **Same network type** (PPO to PPO, HMO to HMO, etc.)
3. **Same actuarial value** (closest to original plan's cost-sharing)
4. **Same deductible range** (similar out-of-pocket structure)
5. **Lowest premium** among qualifying options

**Example Crosswalk:**
```
Original Plan (Discontinued):
BlueCross Silver PPO 205
Metal Tier: Silver
Network: PPO
Deductible: $3,000
Premium: $450/month

Crosswalk Plan (Auto-Assigned):
BlueCross Silver PPO 210
Metal Tier: Silver (same)
Network: PPO (same)
Deductible: $3,500 (similar)
Premium: $475/month (slight increase)

Agent's Note: Close match but higher deductible and premium
```

**When Crosswalks Happen:**
- Annually before Open Enrollment (typically October-November)
- Carriers submit "Plan ID Crosswalk Templates" to CMS
- CMS approves crosswalk mappings
- Clients receive notice in mail (Annual Notice of Change - ANOC)

### Timeline of Passive Renewal Process

**August - September:**
- CMS releases draft plan data for upcoming year
- Carriers finalize which plans to offer
- Plan discontinuations decided

**October 1:**
- Carriers notify enrollees of plan changes
- Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) mailed to affected members
- 60-day notice minimum required

**November 1:**
- Open Enrollment begins
- Clients can actively shop and change plans
- Agents can assist with re-enrollment

**Mid-November:**
- Carriers submit final crosswalk mappings to CMS
- Marketplace systems updated with crosswalk data

**December 15:**
- Deadline for most states to select plan for January 1 effective date
- Clients who don't actively enroll are flagged for passive renewal

**Late December:**
- Passive renewals and crosswalks processed
- Subsidies recalculated based on updated FPL and income

**January 1:**
- New coverage effective for renewed or crosswalked plans
- Clients who didn't enroll are automatically in new plan

### When Passive Renewal Happens Smoothly

**Scenario: Plan Continues, No Changes**

**Client Situation:**
- Enrolled in BlueCross Silver 205 for 2025
- Same plan offered for 2026
- Client takes no action during Open Enrollment

**What Happens:**
1. Client's income and household info from 2025 application automatically rolled forward
2. Subsidy recalculated using 2026 FPL guidelines
3. Automatically renewed in BlueCross Silver 205 for 2026
4. Premium may change (plan rate + updated subsidy)
5. Coverage continues January 1 with no gap

**Agent Involvement:**
- Passive renewal happens without agent involvement
- Agent typically does NOT receive renewal commission (check carrier policy)
- Some carriers pay small passive renewal commission to agent of record

**Example Commission Impact:**
```
Active re-enrollment commission: $20 per member per month (PMPM) × 12 months = $240/year
Passive renewal commission: $0 - $5 PMPM × 12 months = $0 - $60/year

Loss to agent: $180 - $240/year per client if passive
```

⚠️ **AGENT TIP:** Proactively contact clients before Open Enrollment to encourage active re-enrollment and plan review.

### When Crosswalks Occur

**Scenario 1: Plan Discontinued, Same Carrier**

**Client Situation:**
- Enrolled in Oscar Silver Saver 2025
- Oscar discontinues Silver Saver for 2026
- Oscar offers new Silver Smart plan

**What Happens:**
1. Oscar submits crosswalk: Silver Saver → Silver Smart
2. Client receives ANOC notice in October
3. If client doesn't select new plan, automatically crosswalked to Silver Smart on Jan 1
4. Coverage continues with no gap

**Agent Opportunity:**
- Contact client about plan change
- Review new plan details (network, deductible, copays)

- Assist with active re-enrollment = new commission

**What to Check:**
- Is crosswalk plan actually similar?
- Are client's doctors still in network?
- Is deductible similar or higher?
- Is premium lower or higher?
- Are prescriptions still covered at same tier?

```
Original: Oscar Silver Saver PPO

- Network: 5,000 providers (smaller!)
- Deductible: $3,000 (higher!)
- Premium: $375/month (lower)
- Requires PCP and referrals (more restrictive)

Agent Action: Recommend actively shopping for better PPO alternative
```

**1. Client's Plan is Being Discontinued**

### Commission Implications

**Active Re-enrollment Commissions:**
- Treated as new enrollment for commission purposes
- Full first-year commission rate (often $15-$25 PMPM)
- Renewal commissions in subsequent years if client stays on plan

**Passive Renewal Commissions:**
- Varies by carrier
- Some pay NO commission on passive renewals
- Some pay reduced "renewal rate" ($0-$10 PMPM)
- Some pay same as active (rare)

**Crosswalk Commissions:**
- **If same carrier:** Usually treated as renewal (reduced commission)
- **If different carrier:** May not assign commission to any agent (orphaned client)
- **If you actively re-enroll:** Full commission as new enrollment

**Commission Comparison Example:**
```
Client passively renewed:
Year 1: $5 PMPM × 12 = $60
Year 2-10: $5 PMPM × 12 = $60/year
Total 10-year value: $600

Client actively re-enrolled:
Year 1: $20 PMPM × 12 = $240
Year 2-10: $10 PMPM × 12 = $120/year
Total 10-year value: $1,320

Difference: $720 more by active re-enrollment
```

⚠️ **AGENT TIP:** Even if commission is similar, active re-enrollment provides opportunity to:
- Add value to client relationship
- Cross-sell other products (Medicare supplement, life, etc. in future)
- Get referrals
- Retain client loyalty
