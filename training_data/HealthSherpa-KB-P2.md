## Article 3: I Can't Find My Doctor in the Provider Network Search

**Meta Description:** Troubleshoot provider network search issues. Learn how to search by NPI number, verify network participation directly, and understand out-of-network costs.

**Search Terms:**
- Can't find doctor provider network search
- NPI number lookup physician HealthSherpa
- Verify doctor accepts insurance plan
- Provider directory search not working  
- Out of network doctor costs ACA

### Problem Statement
You're searching for your doctor in a health plan's provider network directory on HealthSherpa, but you can't find them. You're worried about choosing a plan that doesn't cover your preferred healthcare providers.

### Understanding Provider Network Directories

Insurance companies contract with specific doctors, hospitals, and other healthcare providers to create a "network." Staying in-network typically means:
- Lower copays and coinsurance
- No balance billing
- Claims processed automatically
- Costs count toward your deductible and out-of-pocket maximum

Out-of-network care typically means:
- Higher costs (often 40-50% coinsurance vs. 20-30% in-network)
- Provider may "balance bill" you for the difference between their charge and what insurance pays
- Costs may not count toward your out-of-pocket maximum (except in HMO/EPO plans where it doesn't count at all)
- You may need to file claims yourself

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** HMO and EPO plans typically provide NO coverage for out-of-network care (except emergencies). PPO and POS plans provide some coverage but at higher cost.

### Step-by-Step Solutions to Find Your Doctor

#### Method 1: Search by Doctor's Name (2-3 minutes)

**Step 1:** From the plan comparison page on HealthSherpa, click "Check if your doctor is covered" on any plan you're considering.

**Step 2:** In the provider search box, enter your doctor's:
- Last name first (Example: "Smith" not "Dr. John Smith")
- Full first and last name
- Practice or clinic name

**Step 3:** Apply filters:
- **Specialty:** Select from dropdown (Primary Care, Cardiology, Dermatology, etc.)
- **Distance:** 5 miles, 10 miles, 25 miles, 50 miles from your ZIP code
- **Provider Type:** Individual Doctor, Group Practice, Hospital, Facility

**Step 4:** Review results:
- Provider name and credentials (MD, DO, NP, PA)
- Specialty
- Office address and phone number
- Accepting new patients status
- Associated medical group or hospital

[SCREENSHOT: Provider search interface with filters]

**If your doctor doesn't appear:**
- Try alternative name spellings (Example: "Jon" vs "John", "Katherine" vs "Catherine")
- Search by their practice name instead (Example: "Knoxville Primary Care Associates")
- Remove filters to expand the search
- Try last name only without first name

#### Method 2: Search by NPI Number (1-2 minutes) - MOST ACCURATE

Every healthcare provider in the United States has a unique 10-digit National Provider Identifier (NPI) number. This is the most reliable search method.

**Step 1: Find Your Doctor's NPI Number**

**Option A: Ask Your Doctor's Office**
- Call the front desk: "What is Dr. [Name]'s NPI number?"
- They can provide it immediately
- Ask for both the individual NPI and group NPI (if applicable)

**Option B: Look Up Online**
1. Visit https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov
2. Click "Search"
3. Enter doctor's first and last name
4. Enter state (Example: Tennessee)
5. Select "Individual" for entity type
6. Click "Search"
7. Review results for correct provider
8. Copy the 10-digit NPI number (Format: 1234567890)

[SCREENSHOT: CMS NPI Registry search results]

**Step 2: Search by NPI in HealthSherpa**
1. Return to the health plan's provider search tool
2. Look for "Search by NPI" field or advanced search options
3. Paste or type the 10-digit NPI number
4. Click "Search"

**Step 3: Verify Results**
- Confirm provider name matches
- Verify office location is correct
- Check if "Accepting new patients" is indicated
- Note the effective date of network participation

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Some providers have TWO NPI numbers:
- **Type 1 (Individual):** The doctor as an individual
- **Type 2 (Organization):** The practice or medical group

If you don't find results with one, try the other. Your doctor may bill under the group NPI.

#### Method 3: Verify Directly with Insurance Company (5-10 minutes) - MOST RELIABLE

Provider directories can be outdated by 30-60 days. For the most current information:

**Step 1: Identify the Insurance Carrier and Plan**
- Note the carrier name (Example: Blue Cross Blue Shield, Ambetter, Oscar, Cigna)
- Note the specific plan name (Example: "BlueCross BlueShield Silver 205")
- Write down the plan ID number shown on HealthSherpa

**Step 2: Call Member Services**
- Find the phone number on the plan details page (usually toll-free, 1-800-XXX-XXXX)
- Call before you enroll: "I'm considering enrolling in [Plan Name]. I need to verify if my doctor participates."
- Provide your doctor's name, office location, and NPI number
- Ask specifically:
  - "Is this doctor in-network for [Plan Name] effective January 1, 2026?"
  - "Is the doctor accepting new patients under this plan?"
  - "Are there any restrictions on referrals or authorizations with this doctor?"

**Step 3: Document the Information**
- Get the representative's name and employee ID
- Ask for a reference number
- Request written confirmation via email if possible
- Write down the date and time of the call

**Step 4: Contact Your Doctor's Office to Confirm**
- Call your doctor's billing department (not reception)
- Ask: "Do you accept [Carrier Name] [Plan Name]?"
- Provide the plan ID number
- Ask: "Will you be in-network effective January 1, 2026?"
- Ask about any changes in network participation

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Get verbal confirmation from BOTH the insurance company and your doctor's office. Network participation can change, and sometimes one party has more current information than the other.

#### Method 4: Use the Carrier's Own Provider Directory (3-5 minutes)

Sometimes HealthSherpa's embedded search tool doesn't have the most updated data. Go to the source:

**Step 1: Find the Carrier's Website**
- From the plan details on HealthSherpa, click "View plan documents" or "Carrier website"

**Step 2: Access the Provider Directory Tool**
- Look for "Find a Doctor," "Provider Search," "Find Care," or similar link
- Select the correct plan type: "ACA Marketplace" or "Individual & Family Plans"
- **Critical:** Do NOT use the Medicare, Medicaid, or Employer plan directory

**Step 3: Enter Your Search Criteria**
- Plan name exactly as shown on HealthSherpa
- Your ZIP code
- Doctor's name or NPI
- Specialty

**Step 4: Download the Full Provider Directory (PDF)**
Most carriers also offer downloadable PDF directories:
- Usually 100-500+ pages
- Use Ctrl+F (PC) or Command+F (Mac) to search for your doctor's name
- PDFs are often updated monthly

#### Method 5: Check Multiple Plan Types

If your doctor isn't in one plan's network, they may be in another plan from the same carrier or a different carrier.

**Compare Networks:**

**Blue Cross Blue Shield Plans:** Often have the largest networks, especially in rural areas
**Ambetter (Centene):** Growing network, strong in community health centers
**Oscar Health:** Smaller network, focused on tech-enabled care and select provider groups
**Cigna:** Mid-sized network, strong in specialty care
**Molina:** Focused on Medicaid networks, more community providers

**Check All Metal Tiers:**
Sometimes a carrier offers different networks for different metal tiers:
- Bronze plans: Sometimes narrower networks
- Silver plans: Standard network
- Gold plans: May have broader networks or premium provider access
- Platinum plans: Often have the broadest networks

**Example:**
```

BlueCross Silver Standard: 12,000 providers in Tennessee  
BlueCross Gold Plus: 15,000 providers in Tennessee
```

### Common Search Errors and Solutions

#### Error: "No Results Found"

**Possible Causes:**
1. Misspelled name
2. Doctor recently joined or left the network
3. Doctor practices under a different legal name
4. Doctor is listed under group practice name only
5. Database hasn't been updated

**Solutions:**
- Try searching by practice name
- Use NPI number instead
- Try just last name with no first name
- Remove all filters and search broadly
- Call carrier to verify (Method 3)

#### Error: "Multiple Providers with Same Name"

**Possible Causes:**
- Common name (Example: "John Smith")
- Multiple doctors in same practice with similar names
- Same doctor at multiple locations

**Solutions:**
- Filter by specialty
- Filter by ZIP code or specific address
- Look for credentials (MD vs DO vs NP vs PA)
- Compare NPI numbers if shown
- Call doctor's office to confirm which listing is correct

#### Error: "Provider No Longer Accepting New Patients"

**Impact:**
- Even if in-network, you may not be able to establish care
- Existing patients usually can continue seeing the doctor

**Solutions:**
- Call the doctor's office directly to confirm (sometimes directories are wrong)
- If confirmed not accepting new patients, ask:
  - When they might reopen to new patients
  - If there's a waitlist you can join
  - Which other providers in the practice ARE accepting new patients
- Consider choosing a different in-network provider
- If you're an existing patient, you can usually keep seeing them

#### Error: "Provider Information Incomplete or Outdated"

**Common Issues:**
- Wrong office address listed
- Old phone number
- Retired doctor still showing as active
- Practice moved or merged with another group

**Solutions:**
- Verify directly with doctor's office
- Check carrier's website for more recent directory
- Look for "Last Updated" date on directory
- Use NPI Registry to verify provider is still active
- Report errors to carrier member services

### Understanding Out-of-Network Costs

If your doctor is not in any plan's network, understand the financial impact:

#### Cost Comparison Example (Annual Healthcare Costs)

**Scenario:** You visit a specialist 4 times per year, have one MRI, and take 2 prescription medications.

**In-Network (PPO Plan):**
```
Specialist visits (4 × $50 copay): $200
MRI with $150 copay: $150
Prescriptions (24 fills × $10 copay): $240
Annual premium: $400/month × 12 = $4,800
---
Total annual cost: $5,390
```

**Out-of-Network (Same PPO Plan):**
```
Specialist visits (4 × $200, plan pays 50%): $400 out-of-pocket
MRI ($1,500, plan pays 50%): $750 out-of-pocket
Prescriptions (may not be covered): $2,880 (estimated retail)
Annual premium: $400/month × 12 = $4,800
---
Total annual cost: $8,830 (+ potential balance billing)
```

**Cost Difference:** $3,440 more per year for out-of-network care

#### HMO vs EPO vs PPO vs POS Network Rules

**HMO (Health Maintenance Organization):**
- MUST use in-network providers (except emergencies)
- OUT-OF-NETWORK = 0% coverage (you pay 100%)
- Requires Primary Care Physician (PCP) selection
- Requires referrals for specialists
- **Best for:** Those with strong local provider networks

**EPO (Exclusive Provider Organization):**
- MUST use in-network providers (except emergencies)
- OUT-OF-NETWORK = 0% coverage (you pay 100%)
- NO PCP required
- NO referrals needed
- **Best for:** Those who want flexibility within network without referrals

**PPO (Preferred Provider Organization):**
- CAN use out-of-network providers (at higher cost)
- OUT-OF-NETWORK = typically 40-60% coverage (you pay 40-60%)
- NO PCP required
- NO referrals needed
- **Best for:** Those who need access to out-of-network specialists

**POS (Point of Service):**
- CAN use out-of-network with referral from PCP
- OUT-OF-NETWORK = typically 50-70% coverage with referral
- REQUIRES PCP
- Referrals needed for specialists
- **Best for:** Those who want PCP coordination but occasional out-of-network access

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Always verify your specific plan's out-of-network benefits. Rules vary by carrier and plan.

### Alternative Strategies If Your Doctor Isn't In-Network

#### Strategy 1: Ask Your Doctor to Join the Network
- Doctors can apply to join insurance networks
- Timeline: usually 60-90 days for credentialing
- Ask office manager: "Would you consider joining [Carrier Name] network?"
- More likely if multiple patients request it

#### Strategy 2: Find an In-Network Alternative
- Ask your current doctor: "Who would you recommend in [Carrier Name] network?"
- Use HealthSherpa's provider search to find doctors with similar specialties
- Read online reviews (Healthgrades, Vitals, Google Reviews)
- Check hospital affiliations
- Consider Nurse Practitioners (NPs) or Physician Assistants (PAs) who may have more availability

#### Strategy 3: Use In-Network for Most Care, Pay Out-of-Pocket for Specialist
- Get in-network PCP for routine care
- Continue seeing out-of-network specialist, but pay cash
- Negotiate cash rates (often 30-50% lower than insurance rates)
- Example: $200 insurance rate vs. $120 cash rate

#### Strategy 4: Check if Doctor Offers Sliding Scale or Payment Plans
- Many doctors offer discounts for self-pay patients
- Ask about payment plans for major procedures
- Look into Health Savings Account (HSA) to pay with pre-tax dollars

#### Strategy 5: Appeal for Single-Case Agreement
If you have a complex medical condition and your specialist is the only qualified provider:

**Step 1:** Enroll in the plan
**Step 2:** Request a "single-case agreement" or "network gap exception"
**Step 3:** Provide documentation:
- Letter from your specialist explaining why they are uniquely qualified
- Medical records showing complexity of condition
- Proof that no in-network provider has equivalent expertise

**Step 4:** Submit to insurance company's appeals department
**Step 5:** If approved, out-of-network doctor is treated as in-network for your care

**Success Rate:** Low (10-20%), but worth trying for serious conditions

**Timeframe:** 30-60 days for decision

### Verifying Hospital and Facility Networks

Don't forget to check hospitals and facilities, not just doctors:

**What to Verify:**
- Hospital where your doctor has admitting privileges
- Surgical centers
- Imaging centers (MRI, CT, X-ray)
- Laboratory facilities
- Urgent care centers
- Physical therapy clinics
- Mental health facilities

**Why It Matters:**
Your doctor may be in-network, but if they only perform surgeries at an out-of-network hospital, you'll face huge bills.

**How to Check:**
1. Ask your doctor: "Which hospitals are you affiliated with?"
2. Search for those hospitals in the plan's provider directory
3. Call hospitals: "Is [Hospital Name] in-network for [Plan Name]?"
4. Verify specific departments (Example: ER, Labor & Delivery, ICU may have different network status)

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** In emergencies, you're covered at any hospital. But for planned procedures, you must use in-network facilities.

### Special Considerations for Specific Provider Types

#### Mental Health Providers
- Behavioral health networks are often separate (and smaller) than medical networks
- Search specifically for: Psychiatrists, Psychologists, LCSWs, LPCs, MFTs
- Verify telehealth availability
- Check if prior authorization is required
- Session limits may apply (Example: 20 sessions per year)

#### Pediatricians and OB/GYNs
- Can be designated as PCP (Primary Care Provider) in most plans
- Check "accepting new patients" and age restrictions
- For pregnancy: Verify hospital delivery privileges
- For pediatrics: Check if they see newborns through age 18

#### Specialists Requiring Frequent Care
- Oncologists, Endocrinologists, Rheumatologists, Cardiologists
- Verify not just network status but ALSO:
  - If they're accepting new patients for your specific condition
  - If they accept your plan for ongoing (not just acute) care
  - Where they perform procedures (hospital network status)

#### Pharmacies
- Check if your preferred pharmacy is in-network (Walgreens, CVS, Kroger, Walmart, local)
- Mail-order pharmacy options (often required for 90-day supplies)
- Specialty pharmacy for high-cost drugs (biologics, cancer meds)

### Provider Network Tips and Best Practices

**Before Enrollment:**
1. Make a list of ALL your current providers (PCP, specialists, pharmacy, hospital)
2. Search for each one in plan directories
3. Call to verify network status for effective date
4. Get confirmation in writing when possible
5. Have a backup plan if networks change

**During the Year:**
1. Verify network status before scheduling appointments
2. Keep documentation of network verification calls
3. Ask for pre-authorization if required
4. Report network directory errors to carrier
5. Check for network changes if you move

**At Renewal:**
1. Re-verify all providers are still in-network
2. Check for network changes in annual notice
3. Compare networks if changing plans
4. Update provider information if you've changed doctors
5. Take advantage of Open Enrollment to switch if needed

### Document Everything

**Keep Records Of:**
- Date you verified provider network status
- Name of representative you spoke with
- Reference or confirmation number
- Which plan and plan ID you asked about
- Provider's NPI number
- Written confirmations

**Why It Matters:**
If you're later told your provider is out-of-network and you receive a large bill, your documentation can support an appeal showing you verified network status before enrolling.

## Article 4: How Do I Report a Life Change and Get Special Enrollment?

**Meta Description:** Learn about all 16+ qualifying life events for ACA Special Enrollment, required documentation for each, and exact steps to report changes for coverage.

**Search Terms:**
- How to report life change ACA Marketplace
- Special enrollment period qualifying events
- Documents needed marriage birth job loss SEP
- Update Marketplace application mid-year
- When does Special Enrollment coverage start

### Problem Statement
You missed Open Enrollment but experienced a major life change (marriage, birth, job loss, move, etc.). You need to understand if you qualify for Special Enrollment Period (SEP), what documentation is required, and how to report the change to enroll in coverage.

### Understanding Special Enrollment Periods (SEP)

**What is a Special Enrollment Period?**
A 60-day window (before and/or after a qualifying life event) when you can enroll in or change Marketplace health insurance outside of the annual Open Enrollment Period.

**Key SEP Rules:**
- Must have a qualifying life event (QLE)
- Must enroll within 60 days of the event (some exceptions allow 60 days before)
- Must provide documentation proving the event occurred
- Coverage effective date varies by event type
- Some events allow retroactive coverage

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** If you don't enroll during your 60-day SEP window, you typically must wait until the next Open Enrollment Period (November 1 - January 15) to get coverage.

### Complete List of Qualifying Life Events (16+ Categories)

#### Category 1: Changes in Household (Adds or Removes Family Members)

**QLE #1: Marriage or Entering Domestic Partnership**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days before or after marriage date

**Documentation Required (choose one):**
- Marriage certificate (certified copy)
- Marriage license with officiant signature
- Court certificate of marriage
- Domestic partnership registration (state-specific)
- Religious marriage certificate (if recognized by state)

**Acceptable File Formats:** PDF, JPEG, JPG, PNG, TIFF, BMP (max 10MB)

**Coverage Effective Date:**
- Enroll by 15th of month = Coverage starts 1st of next month
- Enroll after 15th = Coverage starts 1st of month after next

**Example:**
```
Marriage date: March 10
Enroll by April 15 = Coverage starts May 1
Enroll April 20 = Coverage starts June 1
SEP window closes: May 9 (60 days after March 10)
```

**QLE #2: Birth of a Child**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days before expected due date OR 60 days after birth

**Documentation Required (choose one):**
- Birth certificate (hospital copy acceptable initially)
- Hospital birth record or discharge summary
- Letter from hospital on letterhead with baby's name, date of birth, parent names
- Adoption placement documentation (if adopted at birth)

**Coverage Effective Date:** **RETROACTIVE TO BIRTH DATE**

This is a major advantage - even if you enroll 60 days after birth, coverage goes back to the birthday.

**Example:**
```
Baby born: January 15
You enroll: March 10 (55 days later)
Coverage effective: January 15 (retroactive)
SEP window closes: March 16 (60 days after birth)
```

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** The baby is covered under mother's plan for first 30 days as extension of maternity care. Must add baby to a plan by day 31-60 for continuous coverage. If you delay enrollment until day 45, coverage is still retroactive to birth date, but you may face a gap in immediate access to ID cards.

**What if I have twins or triplets?**
- One birth event = one 60-day SEP
- Add all babies simultaneously
- Coverage is retroactive for all

---

**QLE #3: Adoption or Foster Care Placement**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days before or after placement date

**Documentation Required (choose one):**

**For Adoption:**
- Final adoption decree
- Adoption placement agreement
- Court order for adoption
- Letter from adoption agency on letterhead confirming placement date
- Temporary custody order (while adoption is pending)

**For Foster Care:**
- Foster care placement agreement
- Court order for foster care placement
- Letter from state child welfare agency confirming placement
- Temporary custody order

**Coverage Effective Date:** **RETROACTIVE TO PLACEMENT DATE** (similar to birth)

**Example:**
```
Foster placement: February 1
Enroll: March 15
Coverage effective: February 1 (retroactive)
```

---

**QLE #4: Divorce or Legal Separation (Loss of Coverage)**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days after divorce/separation is finalized

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Divorce itself is NOT a QLE. You only qualify for SEP if the divorce/separation causes you to LOSE health coverage (Example: you were on ex-spouse's employer plan).

**Documentation Required:**

**Proof of Divorce/Separation:**
- Final divorce decree (certified copy)
- Legal separation judgment
- Annulment papers
- Court order

**PLUS Proof of Coverage Loss:**
- COBRA election notice
- Letter from ex-spouse's employer confirming coverage termination
- Insurance termination letter showing coverage end date

**Both documents must be submitted together.**

**Coverage Effective Date:**
- If enrolled during month coverage ends = Coverage starts 1st of next month
- If enrolled after coverage ends = Coverage starts 1st of month following enrollment

---

**QLE #5: Death of Family Member Who Provided Coverage**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days after death

**Documentation Required:**
- Death certificate (certified copy or hospital copy)
- Obituary (some states accept)
- Letter from executor of estate
- PLUS proof of coverage loss (insurance termination letter)

**Coverage Effective Date:** 1st of month following enrollment (can be retroactive if during month of death)

#### Category 2: Loss of Other Health Coverage

**QLE #6: Loss of Job-Based Coverage (Employer Plan)**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days before or after coverage loss date

**Documentation Required (choose one):**
- COBRA election notice (most common and preferred)
- Letter from employer's HR department on company letterhead stating:
  - Employee name
  - Coverage end date
  - Reason for loss (layoff, termination, reduction of hours, etc.)
  - Employer name and contact information
- Final paycheck stub showing health insurance deduction stopped
- Insurance company termination letter

**What if I was fired or quit voluntarily?**
Yes, still qualifies! Any involuntary or voluntary loss of job-based coverage qualifies, including:
- Termination (fired)
- Layoff
- Resignation (quit)
- Reduction in hours making you ineligible
- Employer stopped offering coverage
- Became part-time (no longer eligible)
- COBRA ran out

**What if I'm still working but lost eligibility?**
Also qualifies if:
- Employer dropped coverage for employees
- You reduced hours below eligibility threshold
- Waiting period ended and coverage didn't start
- Dependent aged out of parent's plan

**Coverage Effective Date:**
- Can choose 1st of month coverage ends OR 1st of following month
- No gap in coverage if enrolled before old coverage ends

**Example:**
```
Job ends: October 20
Employer coverage ends: October 31
Enroll by: September 1 - December 29 (60 days before or after Oct 31)
Coverage options: November 1 or December 1 start date
```

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** If you elect COBRA, you can still apply for Marketplace coverage during SEP and drop COBRA. Compare costs - Marketplace with subsidy is often cheaper than COBRA.

**QLE #7: Loss of COBRA Coverage**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days after COBRA ends

**Reasons COBRA Ends:**
- 18-month COBRA period expired
- 36-month COBRA period expired (for dependents)
- Stopped paying COBRA premiums (involuntary termination)
- Became eligible for Medicare

**Documentation Required:**
- COBRA termination notice from insurance company or employer
- Last COBRA payment confirmation
- Letter from employer/plan administrator stating COBRA end date

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Voluntarily dropping COBRA does NOT qualify for SEP. COBRA must end involuntarily (time limit expired or became ineligible) or you stopped paying and it was terminated.

**QLE #8: Loss of Medicaid or CHIP Coverage**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days before or after coverage ends

**Common Reasons:**
- Income increased above Medicaid limits
- No longer eligible due to age
- Moved to a state with different Medicaid rules
- Medicaid redetermination found ineligible
- CHIP coverage ended (child aged out or income increased)

**Documentation Required:**
- Medicaid/CHIP termination letter from state agency
- Redetermination notice showing ineligibility
- Letter from state Medicaid office confirming end date

**Coverage Effective Date:**
- If enrolled during month Medicaid ends = 1st of next month
- No coverage gap if you enroll before Medicaid ends

---

**QLE #9: Loss of Student Health Coverage**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days after coverage ends

**Common Reasons:**
- Graduated
- Withdrew from school
- Reduced credit hours below eligibility threshold (often 12 credits)
- School year ended and no summer coverage
- Student status changed from full-time to part-time

**Documentation Required:**
- Letter from university health center or insurance provider stating coverage end date
- Student insurance termination notice
- Transcript showing graduation or withdrawal date
- Tuition statement showing credit hour reduction

---

**QLE #10: Aging Off Parent's Plan (26th Birthday)**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days before or after 26th birthday

**Documentation Required:**
- Birth certificate showing you're turning 26
- Insurance termination letter from parent's plan
- Letter from parent's insurance confirming dependent coverage end date

**Coverage Effective Date:**
- Enroll before 26th birthday = Coverage starts 1st of month you turn 26 (no gap)
- Enroll after 26th birthday = 1st of month following enrollment

**Typical Coverage End Date on Parent's Plan:**
- Most plans: Coverage ends on your 26th birthday (or last day of month you turn 26)
- Some plans: Coverage continues through end of calendar year you turn 26

**Example:**
```
Birthday: March 15 (turning 26)
Parent's plan ends: March 15 (or March 31, check your plan)
SEP window: January 15 - May 14
Best enrollment timing: By Feb 15 to get March 1 start date
```

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Plan ahead! Don't wait until after your coverage ends. Enroll early to avoid any gap.

#### Category 3: Change in Residence

**QLE #12: Permanent Move to New ZIP Code or County**

**Eligibility Window:** 60 days before or after move date

**What Qualifies as a Permanent Move:**
✅ Moving to a new home (renting or buying)
✅ Moving to or from a college town as a student
✅ Moving for a new job (seasonal workers)
✅ Moving from a shelter or transitional housing to permanent housing
✅ Moving to the U.S. from a foreign country or U.S. territory (Puerto Rico, Guam, etc.)

**What Does NOT Qualify:**
❌ Temporary vacation or travel
❌ Moving to a family member's house for short-term stay
❌ Moving for medical treatment (hospital stay, rehabilitation)
❌ Incarcerated then released to same address

**Documentation Required (choose one):**

**Proof of New Address:**
- Lease agreement or rental contract with start date
- Mortgage statement or property deed
- Utility bill (gas, electric, water) at new address dated within 30 days
- Driver's license or state ID with new address
- Voter registration card with new address
- Letter from shelter, transitional housing, or college confirming residency
- Change of address confirmation from U.S. Postal Service

**PLUS Proof of Prior Coverage (if you had qualifying coverage):**
- Insurance ID card from previous plan
- Last premium statement
- Insurance termination letter
- Letter from previous employer confirming coverage

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** You must have had "minimum essential coverage" (MEC) for at least one day in the 60 days before moving. MEC includes Marketplace plans, employer coverage, Medicaid, Medicare, CHIP, TRICARE, VA coverage, Peace Corps coverage.

**Exception:** If moving from a foreign country or territory, no prior coverage required.

**Coverage Effective Date:**
- Moved by 15th of month AND enrolled by 15th = Coverage starts 1st of next month
- Enrolled after 15th = Coverage starts 1st of month after next

**Example:**
```
Move date: April 10
Old address: Kentucky (had BCBS plan)
New address: Tennessee
Enroll by: April 15 = May 1 start date
Enroll April 20 = June 1 start date
SEP window: February 10 - June 9 (60 days before and after)
```

**Special Rule for Moving to Area with Different Plans:**
Even if you had Marketplace coverage before, you may need to change plans because:
- Your old plan isn't offered in new area
- Different carriers operate in new state/county
- Different plan options and pricing

### What Happens If You Don't Upload Documents in Time?

**30-Day Upload Deadline:**
Most qualifying events require documentation upload within 30 days of enrollment (some allow up to 90 days for certain events).

**If You Miss the Deadline:**

**Outcome 1: Coverage Remains Active Pending Verification (Most Common for Birth/Adoption)**
- Coverage continues temporarily
- You receive extension letters giving additional time
- Must submit within extended deadline (often 60-90 days total)
- If still not submitted: Coverage terminated retroactively to effective date

**Outcome 2: Coverage is Terminated Immediately (Common for Loss of Coverage Events)**
- You receive termination notice
- Effective date of coverage is cancelled
- Must repay any subsidies received
- Must wait until next Open Enrollment to reapply (unless you have another qualifying event)

**Outcome 3: SEP Application Denied (Before Coverage Starts)**
- Enrollment is not processed
- You don't get coverage
- Must wait until Open Enrollment or have another qualifying event

**Example:** You had a baby on December 10 (during Open Enrollment).

**Example:** Lost job coverage June 30, enrolled in a Bronze plan, but now (August) want to switch to Silver plan.

**Can You Switch?**
- NO - Once you've used your SEP and selected a plan, you cannot change plans until next Open Enrollment
- Exception: If you have ANOTHER qualifying event (Example: marriage, move, birth)

**Lesson:** Choose carefully during SEP!

#### Scenario 4: Qualifying Event But Income Changed Significantly

**Example:** Lost job (had $60,000 salary), now unemployed (getting $15,000 unemployment benefits).

**What to Do:**
1. Report loss of coverage as qualifying event
2. ALSO update income to reflect new amount
3. Subsidy will recalculate based on new income
4. May qualify for much higher subsidy OR Medicaid

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Always update income when reporting life changes. This ensures accurate subsidy and avoids repayment at tax time.

**Example:** Lost job coverage March 31, didn't realize you had SEP, now it's June 15.

## Article 5: My Prescription Isn't Showing as Covered

**Meta Description:** Learn how to check drug formularies, understand medication tiers, request exceptions, appeal denials, and access prior authorization for prescriptions.

**Search Terms:**
- Prescription drug not covered health insurance
- How to check formulary ACA plan
- Drug tiers 1 2 3 4 explained
- Prior authorization process medication
- Appeal prescription drug denial

### Problem Statement
You're checking if your prescription medications are covered by a health plan, but your drug isn't showing up in the formulary search, is in a high-cost tier, or requires prior authorization. You need to understand your options.

### Understanding Prescription Drug Formularies

**What is a Formulary?**
A formulary is the list of prescription drugs covered by your health insurance plan. It's organized into "tiers" with different cost-sharing levels.

**Why Do Plans Have Formularies?**
- Negotiate discounts with drug manufacturers
- Encourage use of cost-effective medications
- Promote generic over brand-name drugs when appropriate
- Manage specialty drug costs

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Every ACA Marketplace plan MUST cover at least one drug in each United States Pharmacopeia (USP) category and class. But they don't have to cover EVERY drug, and can put expensive drugs in high tiers.

### Understanding Drug Tiers (1-4 or 1-5)

Most plans use 4-5 tier structures:

**Tier 1: Preferred Generic Drugs**
- Lowest cost
- **Typical Copay:** $5-$15 per fill
- **Coinsurance:** Sometimes 10-20% after deductible
- **Examples:** Metformin (diabetes), Lisinopril (blood pressure), Omeprazole (acid reflux)

**Tier 2: Generic Drugs**
- Low to moderate cost
- **Typical Copay:** $15-$35 per fill
- **Coinsurance:** 20-30% after deductible
- **Examples:** Newer generics, some older generics not on preferred list

**Tier 3: Preferred Brand-Name Drugs**
- Moderate to high cost
- **Typical Copay:** $40-$80 per fill
- **Coinsurance:** 30-40% after deductible
- **Examples:** Brand drugs with generic alternatives, but brand is preferred by plan

**Tier 4: Non-Preferred Brand-Name Drugs**
- High cost
- **Typical Copay:** $80-$150+ per fill
- **Coinsurance:** 40-50% after deductible
- **Examples:** Brand drugs when generic available, newer brand drugs

**Tier 5: Specialty Drugs** (if plan has 5 tiers)
- Very high cost (often $500-$3,000+ per month)
- **Typical Coinsurance:** 25-33% after deductible
- **Examples:** Biologics, cancer drugs, hepatitis C drugs, growth hormones, HIV drugs, multiple sclerosis medications
- Often require specialty pharmacy
- May require prior authorization

**Cost Example:**
```
Drug: Atorvastatin (cholesterol medication)
Tier 1 (Generic): $10 copay
vs.
Drug: Lipitor (brand name for same medication)
Tier 4 (Brand): $100 copay or 40% coinsurance ($200-$300 cost)

Annual savings with generic: $90/month × 12 = $1,080
```

### Step-by-Step: How to Check If Your Drug Is Covered

#### Step 1: Find the Plan's Formulary (3-5 minutes)

**Method A: Through HealthSherpa**
1. While browsing plans on HealthSherpa
2. Click "Check if your prescriptions are covered" on any plan
3. Type medication name in search box
4. Select correct drug, strength, and quantity from dropdown
5. Review tier and cost information

[SCREENSHOT: HealthSherpa drug search interface]

**Method B: Carrier's Website**
1. Note the carrier name and plan name from HealthSherpa
2. Visit carrier's website (Example: www.bcbstn.com)
3. Look for "Pharmacy" or "Find a Drug"
4. Select plan type: "ACA Marketplace" or "Individual & Family"
5. Enter plan name exactly as shown
6. Search for your medication

**Method C: Download Full Formulary PDF**
1. On plan details page, click "Plan Documents"
2. Download "Formulary" or "Prescription Drug List"
3. PDF is usually 50-200 pages
4. Use Ctrl+F (PC) or Command+F (Mac) to search for drug name
5. Check tier and any restrictions

#### Step 2: Search for Your Medication (2 minutes)

**Tips for Searching:**
- Try both generic name AND brand name
  - Example: "atorvastatin" AND "Lipitor"
- Try alternate spellings
- Search by drug class if you can't find specific drug
  - Example: "statins" for cholesterol medications
- Look for combination drugs under each component
  - Example: Advair (fluticasone + salmeterol) might be listed under either ingredient

**Information You'll See:**
- Drug name (generic and/or brand)
- Tier (1-5)

- Prior authorization required? (PA, QL, ST symbols)
- Step therapy required?
- Specialty designation?

#### Step 3: Understand Restrictions and Requirements

**Common Restriction Types:**

**PA - Prior Authorization**
- Doctor must get approval from insurance before prescription is filled
- Must prove medical necessity
- Processing time: 24-72 hours (expedited) or 5-7 days (standard)
- Required for many expensive medications, controlled substances, off-label uses

**QL - Quantity Limits**
- Maximum quantity allowed per fill or per month
- Example: "30 tablets per 30 days" or "1 inhaler per 30 days"
- Based on FDA dosing guidelines
- Can request exception if doctor prescribes higher dose

**ST - Step Therapy**
- Must try lower-cost medications first before insurance covers higher-tier drug
- Example: Must try generic statin before brand-name statin covered
- Must show failed attempt or side effects from first-line drugs
- Doctor can request exception for medical reasons

**SP - Specialty Drug**
- Must be filled at specialty pharmacy (can't use regular Walgreens/CVS)
- Often shipped to home
- Requires more monitoring and coordination
- Usually very expensive ($500-$5,000+ per month)

**Example:**
```
Drug: Humira (autoimmune disease treatment)
Tier: 5 (Specialty)
Restrictions: PA, SP
Cost: 25% coinsurance = $1,500-$2,000 per month
Requirement: Prior authorization + use specialty pharmacy
```

### What To Do When Your Drug Isn't Covered

#### Option 1: Find a Covered Alternative (First Step - Most Common)

**Step 1:** Contact your doctor
- Ask: "Are there any covered alternatives to [drug name] for my condition?"
- Provide your plan's formulary or list of covered drugs in same class

**Step 2:** Check therapeutic alternatives
- Drugs that work similarly but are different medications
- Example: If Nexium (esomeprazole) not covered, try Prilosec (omeprazole) which is covered

**Step 3:** Try the generic version
- If brand name not covered, generic almost always is
- Same active ingredient, FDA-approved, usually 80-90% cheaper
- Example: Lipitor → atorvastatin (generic)

**Cost Savings Example:**
```
Brand Name Drug Not Covered: Pay full retail $300/month
vs.
Generic Alternative Covered (Tier 1): Pay $10 copay

Annual Savings: $3,480
```

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Never switch medications without talking to your doctor first. Some people need specific formulations.

#### Option 2: Request a Formulary Exception (Formal Process)

If you've tried alternatives and they don't work, or your doctor says this specific drug is medically necessary, request an exception.

**When to Request Exception:**
- You've tried covered alternatives and had adverse reactions
- Covered alternatives are ineffective for your condition
- The non-covered drug is the only effective treatment
- You've been stable on this medication for years

**Step-by-Step Exception Request:**

**Step 1: Get Doctor's Support Letter (Most Important)**
Your doctor must write a letter explaining:
- Your diagnosis
- Why this specific drug is medically necessary
- What other drugs you've tried and why they failed or caused side effects
- Medical evidence supporting use of this drug
- Risks of switching medications
- Peer-reviewed studies or clinical guidelines supporting this drug choice

**Template for Doctor Letter:**
```

To: [Insurance Company] Pharmacy Department

```

**Step 2: Submit Exception Request**

**Method A: Doctor Submits (Recommended)**
- Doctor's office calls insurance pharmacy line
- Provides member ID, drug name, supporting information
- Faxes letter of medical necessity

**Method B: You Submit**
1. Download exception request form from insurance company website
2. Doctor completes medical necessity section
3. Attach doctor's letter
4. Fax or mail to pharmacy department address on form
5. Keep copy and confirmation of submission

**Contact Info Usually:**
- Insurance company pharmacy department (find on back of insurance card or website)
- Fax number for prior authorization requests

**Step 3: Follow Up**

**Standard Exception Review Timeline:**
- **72 hours** for standard requests
- **24 hours** for expedited requests (if health could be seriously harmed by delay)

**Check Status:**
- Call pharmacy department with your exception request number
- Check insurance company online portal (some have tracking)
- Ask: "What is the status of exception request #[number]?"

**Possible Outcomes:**

**APPROVED:**
- Drug is covered (usually at Tier 3 level)
- Approval typically valid for plan year or specific duration (Example: 12 months)
- Prescription can be filled

**DENIED:**
- Insurance maintains drug is not medically necessary or suitable alternatives exist
- You receive written denial with reason
- You can appeal (see Option 3 below)

**PENDING - NEED MORE INFORMATION:**
- Insurance requests additional medical records
- May need more documentation of failed drug trials
- Doctor may need to provide more clinical justification

#### Option 3: Appeal the Denial (If Exception Denied)

**Internal Appeal Process (Multiple Levels):**

**Level 1: Internal Appeal to Insurance Company**
- Timeline: Must file within **180 days** of denial
- Your doctor must write even more detailed letter
- Include:
  - Peer-reviewed medical literature supporting drug use
  - Clinical practice guidelines
  - Medical records documenting failed alternatives
  - Statement on how denial harms your health

**How to File:**
1. Get denial letter from insurance company
2. Note appeal deadline (usually 180 days)
3. Review denial reason carefully
4. Doctor writes appeal letter addressing specific denial reasons
5. Attach supporting documentation
6. Submit to address on denial letter (often separate appeals department)

**Decision Timeline:**
- **Standard:** 30 days
- **Expedited:** 72 hours (if urgent)

[SCREENSHOT: Sample appeal letter template]

**Level 2: External Independent Review**
If internal appeal denied:
- Request external review from independent third party
- Reviewer is not affiliated with insurance company
- Must file within **60 days** of internal appeal denial
- Free of charge
- Decision is binding on insurance company

**How to File:**
1. Get internal appeal denial letter
2. Request external review form from insurance company OR state insurance department
3. Complete form
4. Attach all previous documentation
5. Submit to state insurance department or independent review organization

**Decision Timeline:**
- **Standard:** 60 days
- **Expedited:** 72 hours

**Success Rate:** External reviews overturn insurance denials in about **30-40%** of cases

**Level 3: State Insurance Department Complaint (Optional - Can Do Anytime)**
- File complaint with state insurance commissioner
- They may intervene on your behalf
- Can be done in parallel with appeals
- Tennessee: https://www.tn.gov/commerce/insurance/consumer-resources.html

#### Option 4: Use Manufacturer Copay Cards and Patient Assistance Programs

If drug is covered but too expensive:

**Manufacturer Copay Cards:**
- Reduces out-of-pocket cost (often to $0-$15)
- Only for brand-name drugs (not available for generics)
- Only works if drug is covered by insurance (can't use if drug is excluded from formulary)
- Search: "[Drug name] copay card" or visit www.needymeds.org

**How to Use:**
1. Visit drug manufacturer's website
2. Look for "Savings" or "Copay Card" link
3. Enter information, download card
4. Present card at pharmacy with prescription
5. Pharmacy processes through manufacturer program

```

Without Copay Card: $80 copay (Tier 3)
With Manufacturer Copay Card: $10 copay
Annual Savings: $840
```

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Copay card amounts may NOT count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum per federal regulations, depending on plan type.

**Patient Assistance Programs (PAPs):**
For people who can't afford medications even with insurance:
- Free or low-cost medications from manufacturers
- Income-based eligibility (often up to 400-500% of Federal Poverty Level)
- Must apply and provide income documentation

**How to Apply:**
1. Visit www.needymeds.org or www.rxassist.org
2. Search for your medication
3. Find manufacturer's patient assistance program
4. Download application
5. Doctor completes provider section
6. Submit with income documentation (tax return, pay stubs)

**Processing Time:** 2-6 weeks
**Duration:** Usually 12 months, then must reapply

#### Option 5: Use GoodRx or Other Discount Cards (When Insurance Doesn't Help)

If drug not covered OR cash price is cheaper than insurance:

**GoodRx:**
- Free discount card/app
- Shows cash prices at pharmacies near you
- No eligibility requirements
- Visit www.goodrx.com

**How to Use:**
1. Download GoodRx app or visit website
2. Search for your medication (name, strength, quantity)
3. Enter ZIP code
4. Compare prices at different pharmacies

- Cost does NOT count toward out-of-pocket maximum
- You're paying cash, not using insurance

**Other Discount Options:**

### Understanding Prior Authorization Process

If your drug is covered but requires PA (Prior Authorization):

**What is Prior Authorization?**
Insurance company requires doctor to get approval before prescription is covered, proving the drug is medically necessary.

**Prior Authorization Process (5-7 Days Standard):**

**Step 1: Doctor Initiates**
- You take prescription to pharmacy
- Pharmacy tries to fill, gets rejection: "Prior authorization required"
- Pharmacy notifies you and doctor

**Step 2: Doctor Submits PA Request**
- Doctor's office calls insurance company PA line or submits form online
- Provides:
  - Diagnosis codes
  - Drug name, strength, quantity
  - Medical justification
  - Documentation of medical necessity
  - Previous treatments tried (if applicable)

**Step 3: Insurance Reviews**
- Pharmacy department reviews request
- May request additional medical records
- May require specific lab values or test results
- Compares to coverage criteria

**Step 4: Decision**

**APPROVED:**
- PA approval code issued
- Usually valid for 1 year (must renew annually)
- Prescription can be filled
- You pay normal copay for that tier

**DENIED:**
- Insurance says not medically necessary or doesn't meet criteria
- Doctor can provide more information or appeal
- You can pay cash for medication
- You can try alternatives

**EXPEDITED PA:**
If you need medication urgently:
- Doctor can request expedited review (24-72 hours)
- Must explain how delay could seriously harm health
- Example: Cancer medication, urgent infection treatment

**What Drugs Commonly Require PA?**
- Expensive specialty medications
- Controlled substances (opioids, stimulants for ADHD)
- High-risk medications
- Drugs with potential for abuse
- Expensive drugs when cheaper alternatives exist
- Off-label uses (drug used for condition not FDA-approved for)

### Step Therapy: Trying Cheaper Drugs First

**What is Step Therapy?**
Requirement to try lower-cost medications before insurance covers higher-cost drugs.

**Common Step Therapy Examples:**

**Cholesterol Medications:**
```
Step 1: Generic statin (atorvastatin, simvastatin) - Covered
↓ (If fails or side effects)
Step 2: Brand statin (Crestor) - Covered with documentation
↓ (If fails or side effects)
Step 3: PCSK9 inhibitor (Repatha) - Covered with PA and step therapy documentation
```

**How to Navigate Step Therapy:**

**Option A: Complete the Steps**
- Try Step 1 drug first (usually generic, low-cost)
- If it doesn't work or causes side effects, doctor documents
- Insurance then approves Step 2 drug
- Process repeats for Step 3

**Timeline:** 30-90 days per step (depending on how long each medication trial takes)

**Option B: Request Step Therapy Exception**
If you've already tried required medications in the past or have medical reason to skip steps:

**Doctor must document:**
- Previous unsuccessful trials of Step 1 and Step 2 drugs (provide dates, results)
- Medical contraindications (reasons you can't take lower-step drugs)
- Urgency (why waiting for step therapy could harm you)

**Submit to:** Insurance company's pharmacy department with exception request

**Decision time:** 72 hours standard, 24 hours expedited

### Specialty Pharmacies for Specialty Drugs

**What are Specialty Drugs?**
High-cost medications (typically >$600/month) for complex chronic conditions:
- Cancer (chemotherapy, immunotherapy)
- Autoimmune diseases (Humira, Enbrel, Remicade)
- Multiple sclerosis (Copaxone, Tysabri)
- Hepatitis C (Harvoni, Sovaldi)
- HIV (antiretrovirals)
- Growth hormone deficiencies
- Rare genetic disorders

**Why Specialty Pharmacy Required?**
- Special handling and storage (refrigeration)
- Patient education and support
- Medication therapy management
- Side effect monitoring
- Injection training
- Insurance coordination

**How Specialty Pharmacy Works:**

**Step 1:** Doctor prescribes specialty medication and sends to specialty pharmacy
- Can't fill at regular pharmacy like Walgreens or CVS
- Insurance assigns specialty pharmacy OR doctor can choose from network

**Step 2:** Specialty pharmacy contacts you
- Coordinates shipment
- Schedules delivery (requires signature)
- Provides education on medication use
- Reviews side effects and administration

**Step 3:** Medication shipped to home
- Overnight or 2-day delivery
- Temperature-controlled packaging
- Usually 30-day supply

**Step 4:** Ongoing support
- Refill reminders
- Nurse hotline for questions
- Injection training (if applicable)
- Side effect monitoring

**Major Specialty Pharmacies:**
- Accredo (Express Scripts/Cigna network)
- CVS Specialty (Caremark network)
- OptumRx Specialty
- AllianceRx Walgreens Prime
- BriovaRx (Diplomat)

**Cost:**
- Usually Tier 5: 25-33% coinsurance
- Example: $3,000 drug × 25% = $750 patient cost
- May hit out-of-pocket maximum quickly
- Check for manufacturer copay assistance programs

### Formulary Changes Mid-Year

**Can plans remove drugs from formulary mid-year?**

**Yes, in limited circumstances:**
- New generic becomes available (brand may be removed)
- FDA safety alert or recall
- Drug withdrawn from market
- Fraud or abuse concerns

**Notice Requirements:**
- 60 days advance notice for non-urgent changes
- Immediate for safety concerns

**Your Rights:**
- If your drug is removed, you can request exception to continue
- Transition supply: Plan must provide 30-day supply while you transition to alternative
- File complaint with state insurance department if improper mid-year change

### Tips for Managing Prescription Costs

**Before Choosing a Plan:**
1. Make a complete list of ALL medications you take (including over-the-counter if expensive)
2. Check each drug in every plan's formulary
3. Note tier and restrictions for each
4. Calculate annual drug costs: Monthly copay × 12
5. Compare total costs (premium + drug costs) across plans
6. Choose plan with best coverage for YOUR specific medications

**During the Year:**
1. Ask doctor about generics for every new prescription
2. Use 90-day mail order for maintenance medications (often cheaper)
3. Check GoodRx before filling expensive prescriptions
4. Apply for manufacturer assistance for brand drugs
5. Keep documentation of all drug costs for tax deductions (if meet threshold)
6. Set up auto-refills to avoid lapses

**At Renewal:**
1. Check if formulary changed (plans must notify you)
2. Verify your drugs are still covered at same tier
3. Look for new generics that became available
4. Compare to other plans if your drugs were moved to higher tiers

### Common Setup Issues and Solutions

**Issue: NPN Not Verifying**

**Causes:**
- NPN entered incorrectly (missing digit, wrong number)
- License not yet active in NIPR system
- Name mismatch between license and HealthSherpa registration

**Solutions:**
- Verify NPN at www.nipr.com (PDB - Producer Database)
- Check for typos - NPN is exactly 10 digits
- Ensure name matches exactly (including middle initial)
- Contact state insurance department if license not showing in NIPR
- Wait 48 hours after license issuance for NIPR database to update

---

**Issue: FFM Certificate Not Accepted**

**Causes:**
- Certificate expired
- Name on certificate doesn't match registration
- Wrong NPN on certificate
- File corrupted or unreadable

**Solutions:**
- Check expiration date (must be current year, not expired past Oct 31)
- Ensure name spelling matches exactly
- Verify NPN number matches
- Re-download certificate from MLMS as fresh PDF
- Clear browser cache and re-upload

---

**Issue: E&O Insurance Not Accepted**

**Causes:**
- Coverage amount too low
- Policy not yet effective
- Policy already expired
- Certificate doesn't show agent's name
- File quality too poor to read

**Solutions:**
- Verify minimum coverage ($100,000 recommended)
- Check effective date hasn't passed yet
- Request new certificate if expired
- Ensure your name (not agency name) is listed as insured
- Request high-quality PDF from E&O provider

---

**Issue: Carrier Appointment Delayed**

**Causes:**
- Background check issues
- Missing carrier-specific requirements
- Incomplete contracting
- Carrier processing backlog

**Solutions:**
- Check email for carrier requests for additional information
- Complete carrier training modules promptly
- Follow up directly with carrier contracting department
- Be patient during peak season (October-December) - may take longer

---

**Issue: Cannot Access Certain Features**

**Causes:**
- Account still pending approval
- Missing requirements (FFM, E&O, appointments)
- Technical browser issues

**Solutions:**
- Check account status - may still be "Pending Verification"
- Verify all required documents uploaded and approved
- Clear browser cache and cookies
- Try different browser (Chrome recommended)
- Contact HealthSherpa support
