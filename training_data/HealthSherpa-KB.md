## Article 1: How Do I Start Shopping for Health Insurance on HealthSherpa?

**Meta Description:** Learn how to start shopping for ACA health insurance on HealthSherpa. Get step-by-step instructions, required documents, and timeline information.

**Search Terms:** 
- Start shopping health insurance HealthSherpa
- How to use HealthSherpa for ACA
- Begin health insurance application online
- HealthSherpa enrollment guide

### Problem Statement
You need health insurance and want to use HealthSherpa to shop for and enroll in an ACA (Affordable Care Act) Marketplace plan, but you're not sure where to start or what information you'll need.

### Step-by-Step Solution

#### What You'll Need Before Starting (15-20 Minutes to Gather)

**Personal Information:**
- Social Security Number (SSN) for everyone applying
- Date of birth for all household members
- Current residential address (ZIP code determines available plans)

**Income Documentation:**
- Most recent pay stubs (last 30-60 days)
- W-2 forms from previous tax year
- Tax return (Form 1040) from last year
- Self-employment records if applicable
- Unemployment benefits statements
- Social Security income statements
- Pension or retirement income documentation

**Current Coverage Information (if applicable):**
- Employer coverage details
- Medicaid/Medicare information
- COBRA information
- Current policy termination dates

**Immigration Documents (for non-citizens):**
- Permanent Resident Card (Green Card)
- Naturalization Certificate
- Work permits with expiration dates
- I-94 number

#### Step 1: Access HealthSherpa (1 minute)
1. Visit **www.healthsherpa.com** using a supported browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge)
2. You'll see the main shopping page with a ZIP code entry field
3. No account creation is required to browse plans

[SCREENSHOT: HealthSherpa homepage with ZIP code entry field highlighted]

#### Step 2: Enter Your ZIP Code (1 minute)
1. Type your 5-digit residential ZIP code in the search box
2. Click "Get Started" or press Enter
3. The system will identify your county and available health plans

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Use your residential ZIP code, not a P.O. Box or work address. Your ZIP code determines which health plans and doctors are available to you, and affects subsidy calculations.

#### Step 3: Enter Household Information (3-5 minutes)
You'll be asked to provide:

**For Each Person Applying:**
- Full legal name (as it appears on Social Security card)
- Date of birth (MM/DD/YYYY format)
- Gender
- Tobacco use in the last 6 months (Yes/No)
- Relationship to you (self, spouse, child, etc.)

[SCREENSHOT: Household members entry screen]

**Success Message:** "Great! We found [X] plans available in your area."

#### Step 4: Enter Income Information (3-5 minutes)
1. Select your tax filing status:
   - Single
   - Married Filing Jointly
   - Married Filing Separately
   - Head of Household
   
2. Enter your annual household income estimate for the current year:
   - Include wages, salaries, tips
   - Add self-employment income
   - Include Social Security benefits (non-SSI)
   - Add investment income, rental income
   - Include alimony received (for divorces finalized before 2019)
   - Add unemployment benefits
   
3. Enter the number of people you'll claim as tax dependents

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Your subsidy amount is calculated using Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI), not your take-home pay. MAGI = Adjusted Gross Income + nontaxable Social Security + tax-exempt interest + foreign income.

[SCREENSHOT: Income entry screen with example amounts]

#### Step 5: Review Available Plans (5-10 minutes)
You'll see plans organized by metal tier:

**Bronze Plans:** Lowest monthly premium, highest out-of-pocket costs, 60% actuarial value
**Silver Plans:** Mid-range premium and costs, 70% actuarial value, qualifies for Cost-Sharing Reductions if income under 250% FPL
**Gold Plans:** Higher premium, lower out-of-pocket costs, 80% actuarial value
**Platinum Plans:** Highest premium, lowest out-of-pocket costs, 90% actuarial value

For each plan, you'll see:
- Monthly premium BEFORE subsidy
- Your estimated monthly premium AFTER subsidy
- Annual deductible
- Maximum out-of-pocket limit
- Primary care copay
- Specialist copay
- Emergency room costs
- Generic prescription costs

[SCREENSHOT: Plan comparison view showing different metal tiers]

#### Step 6: Use Decision Tools (10-15 minutes)

**Doctor Search:**
1. Click "Check if your doctor is covered" on any plan
2. Enter doctor's name or practice name
3. OR search by NPI number (10-digit provider identifier)
4. Filter results by specialty and distance

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** If your doctor doesn't appear, try:
- Alternative spellings of their name
- Their practice/clinic name
- Their 10-digit NPI number (find at npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov)
- Calling the doctor's office to verify network participation

**Prescription Drug Search:**
1. Click "Check if your prescriptions are covered"
2. Enter medication name
3. Select strength and quantity
4. View which tier the drug is on (1-4)
5. See estimated costs per fill

**Cost Calculator:**
1. Estimate annual doctor visits
2. Estimate prescription fills per year
3. Estimate specialist visits
4. Tool calculates total estimated annual costs including premiums + out-of-pocket

#### Step 7: Select Your Plan (2 minutes)
1. Click "Select Plan" on your chosen plan
2. You'll proceed to the full application
3. Review plan details one more time before continuing

#### Step 8: Complete Full Application (10-15 minutes)

**Identity Verification:**
You may be asked questions about your financial history to verify your identity (similar to credit bureau questions):
- Previous addresses
- Auto loan lenders
- Mortgage information

If you fail identity verification:
1. You'll receive a reference number
2. Call Experian Help Desk: 1-866-578-5409
3. OR upload identity documents (driver's license, passport, Social Security card, birth certificate)

**Citizenship/Immigration Status:**
- U.S. Citizen
- U.S. National
- Lawfully Present Immigrant (will need immigration document numbers)

**Other Coverage:**
Indicate if anyone is eligible for:
- Employer coverage
- Medicare
- Medicaid
- TRICARE
- VA benefits

**Authorized Representative (Optional):**
If you're working with an insurance agent or broker, their information may be pre-filled. Verify this is correct.

#### Step 9: Review and Submit (3-5 minutes)
1. Review all information for accuracy
2. Electronically sign the application
3. Agree to terms and conditions
4. Click "Submit Application"

**Success Message:** "Your application has been submitted! Application ID: [number]"

#### Step 10: Make First Premium Payment (5-10 minutes)
1. You'll be redirected to carrier payment portal
2. Payment methods typically accepted:
   - Credit card (Visa, Mastercard, Discover, Amex)
   - Debit card
   - Electronic check (routing and account numbers)
   - Some carriers accept PayPal
   
3. First payment amount = Your premium for coverage month + any additional months to effective date
4. Set up auto-pay to avoid missing payments

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Your coverage is NOT active until you pay your first premium. This is called "effectuation." Payment must be received by the deadline shown (typically 15th of the month for coverage starting the 1st of next month).

**Success Message:** "Payment received! Your coverage will be effective [date]. You'll receive an ID card from [carrier name] within 10-15 business days."

### Common Error Messages and Solutions

**Error: "We couldn't verify your identity"**
- **Cause:** Information doesn't match credit bureau records or you have limited credit history
- **Solution:** Call Experian Help Desk at 1-866-578-5409 with your reference number OR upload documents proving identity
- **Documents Accepted:** Driver's license, state ID, passport, Social Security card, birth certificate, tribal ID
- **Processing Time:** 7-10 business days after document upload

**Error: "Your estimated income qualifies you for Medicaid"**
- **Cause:** Income entered is below 138% of Federal Poverty Level in Medicaid expansion state
- **Solution:** You'll be redirected to your state Medicaid application. Complete Medicaid application instead.
- **Note:** Medicaid has no premiums and provides comprehensive coverage.

**Error: "No plans available in your area"**
- **Cause:** ZIP code may be incorrect or service temporarily unavailable
- **Solution:** Verify ZIP code is your residential address. Try again in a few hours. Call HealthSherpa support if persists.

**Error: "Income verification needed"**
- **Cause:** Projected income differs significantly from last year's tax return
- **Solution:** You'll need to submit recent pay stubs, W-2, or other income documentation within 90 days
- **Don't worry:** Coverage continues while verification is pending

## Article 2: Why Is My Subsidy Amount Different Than I Expected?

**Meta Description:** Understand how ACA subsidies are calculated, why your premium tax credit differs from estimates, and how to update income for accurate savings.

**Search Terms:**
- Why subsidy amount different HealthSherpa
- Premium tax credit calculation ACA
- Update income mid year Marketplace
- MAGI calculation health insurance
- Subsidy reconciliation tax return

### Problem Statement
You received your subsidy determination and the amount is higher or lower than you expected, or your subsidy changed after updating your application. You need to understand how subsidies are calculated and how to ensure you receive the correct amount.

### Understanding the Subsidy Calculation Formula

Your premium tax credit (subsidy) is calculated using this exact formula:

**Premium Tax Credit = (Benchmark Premium) - (Expected Contribution)**

Where:
- **Benchmark Premium** = Cost of second-lowest-cost Silver plan in your area
- **Expected Contribution** = Your household MAGI × Contribution Percentage (based on Federal Poverty Level)

#### Step 1: Determine Your Federal Poverty Level (FPL) Percentage

Your subsidy eligibility is based on your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) as a percentage of the Federal Poverty Level for your household size.

**2025 Federal Poverty Levels (48 Contiguous States & DC):**

| Household Size | 100% FPL | 150% FPL | 200% FPL | 250% FPL | 300% FPL | 400% FPL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15,060 | $22,590 | $30,120 | $37,650 | $45,180 | $60,240 |
| 2 | $20,440 | $30,660 | $40,880 | $51,100 | $61,320 | $81,760 |
| 3 | $25,820 | $38,730 | $51,640 | $64,550 | $77,460 | $103,280 |
| 4 | $31,200 | $46,800 | $62,400 | $78,000 | $93,600 | $124,800 |
| 5 | $36,580 | $54,870 | $73,160 | $91,450 | $109,740 | $146,320 |
| 6 | $41,960 | $62,940 | $83,920 | $104,900 | $125,880 | $167,840 |

*Add $5,380 for each additional person*

**Alaska FPL is approximately 25% higher**
**Hawaii FPL is approximately 15% higher**

#### Step 2: Calculate Your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI)

MAGI is NOT the same as your gross income or take-home pay. Here's the exact calculation:

**Start with your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) from Line 11 of IRS Form 1040**

**ADD:**
- Nontaxable Social Security benefits (including SSDI, but NOT SSI)
- Tax-exempt interest income (usually from municipal bonds)
- Excluded foreign-earned income and housing expenses for Americans abroad

**DO NOT INCLUDE:**
- Supplemental Security Income (SSI)
- Child support received
- Gifts and inheritances
- Workers' compensation
- Veterans benefits (except if you're taxed on them)
- Most disaster relief payments

**SUBTRACT (these reduce your MAGI):**
- Pre-tax 401(k) or 403(b) contributions
- Traditional IRA contributions (not Roth)
- Health Savings Account (HSA) contributions
- Self-employment tax deduction
- Student loan interest deduction
- Alimony paid (for divorces finalized before 2019)
- Educator expenses deduction

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** The following DO count as income for MAGI:
- Unemployment benefits (100% taxable)
- Taxable Social Security benefits
- Investment income (dividends, capital gains)
- Rental property income
- Self-employment income (after business expenses)
- Pensions and IRA distributions
- Taxable scholarships and grants
- Alimony received (for divorces before 2019)

**MAGI Example:**
```
AGI from Form 1040: $45,000
+ Nontaxable Social Security: $3,000
+ Tax-exempt interest: $500
= MAGI: $48,500

For household of 2 in 2025:
$48,500 ÷ $20,440 (100% FPL) = 237% FPL
```

[SCREENSHOT: MAGI calculation worksheet]

#### Step 3: Determine Your Expected Contribution Percentage

Under the Affordable Care Act (as enhanced by the American Rescue Plan and Inflation Reduction Act through 2025), your required contribution is capped:

**2025 Contribution Percentages (Percentage of MAGI you pay):**

| Income (% FPL) | Max % of Income for Benchmark Plan |
|---|---|
| 0-150% | 0-2% |
| 150-200% | 2-4% |
| 200-250% | 4-6% |
| 250-300% | 6-8.5% |
| 300%+ | 8.5% |

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** After 2025, these percentages may change if legislation is not renewed. Check current-year guidelines.

**Example Calculation:**
```
Household: 2 people
Annual MAGI: $48,500
FPL Percentage: 237%
Contribution Rate: ~5.5% (interpolated between 200-250% range)

Expected Contribution: $48,500 × 0.055 = $2,668 per year = $222/month

Benchmark Premium (2nd-lowest Silver): $850/month

Premium Tax Credit: $850 - $222 = $628/month
```

[SCREENSHOT: Contribution percentage chart]

#### Step 4: Identify the Benchmark Plan

The benchmark is ALWAYS the second-lowest-cost Silver plan available to your household in your county, NOT:
- The cheapest plan available
- The plan you selected
- A Bronze or Gold plan
- A plan from a specific carrier

**Why This Matters:**
- If you choose a Bronze plan cheaper than your subsidy, the extra subsidy applies, potentially making your premium $0
- If you choose a Gold plan more expensive than benchmark, you pay the full difference
- Benchmark premium varies by ZIP code, age, and tobacco use

**Example:**
```
Available Silver Plans in Your Area (Age 35, Non-Tobacco):
Plan A: $780/month
Plan B: $850/month ← Benchmark (2nd-lowest)
Plan C: $875/month
Plan D: $920/month

Your subsidy: Based on $850/month benchmark
If you choose Plan A ($780): Your cost = $222 - $780 = $0* (plus $558 wasted subsidy)
If you choose Plan B ($850): Your cost = $222/month
If you choose Plan D ($920): Your cost = $292/month

*Note: Some carriers may still charge a small minimum premium
```

### Common Reasons Your Subsidy Differs From Expectations

#### Reason 1: Income Projection vs. Actual Income

**Scenario:** You estimated $40,000 annual income during enrollment, but you're actually making $50,000.

**Impact:** Higher income = higher expected contribution = lower subsidy

**Solution:**
1. Update your Marketplace application immediately
2. Log into your account or call Healthcare.gov: 1-800-318-2596
3. Report the income change
4. Your subsidy will be recalculated
5. Your monthly premium will increase prospectively

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** You MUST reconcile subsidies on your tax return (Form 8962). If you received too much subsidy, you'll owe money back. If too little, you'll receive additional credit.

**Repayment Caps (if you received too much subsidy):**

| Income Level (% FPL) | Single Filer | All Other Filers |
|---|---|---|
| Under 200% | $350 | $700 |
| 200-300% | $900 | $1,800 |
| 300-400% | $1,500 | $3,000 |
| 400%+ | No cap | No cap (full repayment) |

#### Reason 2: Household Size Changes

**Scenario:** You enrolled as household of 2 (you + spouse = $20,440 FPL). You had a baby, now household of 3 ($25,820 FPL).

**Impact:** Same income spread across higher FPL = lower FPL percentage = higher subsidy

**Solution:**
1. Report the change within 30 days
2. Update application with new household member
3. Add baby to your health plan (60-day Special Enrollment Period)
4. Subsidy recalculates automatically

#### Reason 3: Age-Based Premium Increases

**Scenario:** Your subsidy decreased even though income stayed the same.

**Cause:** Insurance premiums increase with age (rates can triple from age 21 to 64). However, if your subsidy is based on benchmark premium, and benchmark increased with your age, your subsidy increases too.

**Impact:** Generally neutral—both your premium and subsidy increase proportionally.

#### Reason 4: Tobacco Use Declaration

**Scenario:** You indicated tobacco use during enrollment.

**Impact:** Premiums can be up to 50% higher for tobacco users. Benchmark premium includes tobacco surcharge, so subsidy is calculated on the higher amount, but you pay the higher premium too.

**Net Effect:** You pay more overall, but subsidy is also higher.

#### Reason 5: Geography and Plan Availability

**Scenario:** You moved from a rural area (fewer plans, higher benchmark) to urban area (more competition, lower benchmark).

**Impact:** Lower benchmark = lower subsidy, but also lower premiums overall.

**Solution:** When you move, you qualify for Special Enrollment Period. Benchmark recalculates based on new ZIP code.

#### Reason 6: State vs. Federal Marketplace Differences

**Scenario:** You moved from a state-based Marketplace to Healthcare.gov (or vice versa).

**Impact:** Calculation method is the same, but plan availability differs. State marketplaces may have additional plan options.

#### Reason 7: Data Matching Issues (DMI)

**Scenario:** Your projected income is significantly lower than last year's tax return, or IRS has no tax data for you.

**Impact:** Marketplace flags your application for income verification. Your subsidy may be adjusted to last year's tax data until you submit documentation.

**Solution:**
1. Submit income verification documents within 90 days
2. Acceptable documents:
   - Recent pay stubs (last 30-60 days)
   - Letter from employer on company letterhead
   - Profit/loss statement for self-employed
   - Unemployment benefits letter
   - Social Security benefits letter
   - Pension/retirement income statements
3. Upload to HealthCare.gov account under "Documents"
4. Subsidy will adjust once documents are processed (7-10 business days)

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** If you don't submit documents within 90 days, your subsidy will be terminated and you'll have to pay full premium until Open Enrollment or qualify for Special Enrollment Period.

### How to Update Your Income Mid-Year

**When to Report Income Changes:**
- New job or job loss
- Pay raise or reduction
- Change from full-time to part-time (or vice versa)
- Start or stop of self-employment
- Change in marital status affecting income
- New investment income or rental property
- Unemployment benefits start/end
- Social Security income change

**How to Report (Choose One Method):**

**Method 1: Online (Fastest)**
1. Log into HealthCare.gov or your state Marketplace
2. Select your current year application
3. Click "Report a Life Change" or "Update Application"
4. Select "Income change"
5. Enter new projected annual income
6. Review updated subsidy amount
7. Electronically sign and submit

**Processing Time:** Immediate recalculation, effective next month

**Method 2: By Phone**
1. Call Marketplace: 1-800-318-2596 (TTY: 1-855-889-4325)
2. Have your Application ID ready
3. Report new income amount
4. Representative will update and provide new subsidy amount

**Processing Time:** Immediate, confirmation mailed within 10 days

**Method 3: Through Your Agent/Broker**
1. Contact your HealthSherpa agent
2. Provide updated income information
3. Agent will submit updated application on your behalf

**Processing Time:** Same day, you receive email confirmation

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Income updates take effect the first of the month following the update. Example: Update submitted March 15 = New premium effective April 1.

### The Reconciliation Process: What Happens at Tax Time

Every year, you MUST reconcile your premium tax credit on your federal tax return using Form 8962.

**Forms You'll Need:**
- **Form 1095-A:** Sent by Marketplace in January, shows:
  - Monthly premiums for your plan
  - Monthly premium for benchmark plan  
  - Advance premium tax credit (APTC) paid each month
  - Months you had coverage
- **Form 8962:** Premium Tax Credit reconciliation form you complete
- **Form 1040:** Your federal tax return
- **IRS Form 1040 Schedule 3:** Where final credit/repayment is reported

**Reconciliation Steps:**
1. Download Form 1095-A from your Marketplace account in January
2. Use tax software or tax preparer to complete Form 8962
3. Calculate actual premium tax credit based on final MAGI
4. Compare actual credit to advance credit received

**Three Possible Outcomes:**

**Outcome 1: You Received the Correct Amount**
- No additional credit or repayment
- Line 26 of Form 8962 = $0

**Outcome 2: You Received Too Little**
- You get additional credit (refund or reduced taxes owed)
- Example: You qualified for $8,000 credit but only received $6,000
- You receive $2,000 additional credit on your tax return

**Outcome 3: You Received Too Much**
- You must repay excess (subject to repayment caps)
- Example: You received $8,000 credit but only qualified for $5,000
- You may owe up to $3,000 (or capped amount based on income)

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** If you don't file taxes and reconcile your premium tax credit, you won't be able to receive advance premium tax credits in future years until you file and reconcile.

### IRS Forms That Matter for Your Subsidy

**Form 1095-A:**
- Available in your Marketplace account in January
- Mailed by February 15
- If incorrect, request corrected form immediately
- Don't file taxes until you have correct 1095-A

**Form 8962:**
- Premium Tax Credit calculation worksheet
- Required if you received any advance premium tax credit
- Calculates final credit amount
- Determines repayment or additional credit

**Form 1040:**
- Main tax return
- Line 30: Reports premium tax credit from Form 8962
- Your final MAGI from Form 1040 determines eligibility

**Schedule 1 (Form 1040):**
- Reports additional income that affects MAGI
- Line 8: Unemployment compensation
- Schedule C profit/loss for self-employed

### Special Situations

**Situation 1: Married Filing Separately**

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** If you're married and file separately (not due to domestic abuse or abandonment), you generally cannot receive premium tax credits UNLESS:
- You meet the domestic abuse/abandonment exception
- You lived apart from spouse for full year
- You can prove separate household

Alternative: File Married Filing Jointly to maintain subsidy eligibility.

**Situation 2: Claimed as a Dependent**

If someone can claim you as a tax dependent (even if they don't):
- Your income is NOT counted for your own subsidy
- Their income is counted for the household MAGI
- You cannot apply separately from the person who can claim you

**Situation 3: Self-Employment Income Fluctuations**

For self-employed individuals:
- Project your income based on year-to-date profit/loss
- Update quarterly as business income changes
- Remember: MAGI = Self-employment income MINUS business expenses
- Consider quarterly income updates to avoid large repayments

**Situation 4: Unemployment to Employment Transition**

When you become unemployed:
- Update Marketplace application immediately
- Project annual income based on unemployment benefits + any remaining employment
- May qualify for higher subsidy or Medicaid
- When reemployed, update again within 30 days

**Situation 5: 400% FPL Cliff (Through 2025)**

Under current law (through 2025):
- No one pays more than 8.5% of income for benchmark plan
- After 2025, the 400% FPL cap may return
- Those over 400% FPL may lose all subsidies

Planning: If you're close to 400% FPL threshold and law changes, consider:
- Maximizing pre-tax retirement contributions
- HSA contributions
- Other MAGI-reducing strategies

### Troubleshooting: "Why did my subsidy change?"

**Check These First:**

1. **Log into your Marketplace account:**
   - Review current application
   - Check for any notices or alerts
   - Look for Data Matching Issues (DMI)

2. **Verify your information is current:**
   - Household size correct?
   - Income projection accurate?
   - Address up to date?

3. **Check for policy changes:**
   - Did benchmark plan premium increase?
   - Did Federal Poverty Level guidelines change?
   - Are you now a year older (age-based increases)?

4. **Review your monthly statement:**
   - Compare premium from last month
   - Check subsidy amount line-by-line
   - Look for carrier rate changes

**Common Error Messages:**

**"Your subsidy has been adjusted due to income verification"**
- **Cause:** DMI was resolved with different income than projected
- **Solution:** Review what documents were used. Appeal if incorrect within 30 days.

**"You are no longer eligible for premium tax credit"**
- **Cause:** Income now exceeds 400% FPL (if law changed) or did not reconcile previous year
- **Solution:** File previous tax return with Form 8962, or report corrected income

**"Your subsidy decreased due to plan changes"**
- **Cause:** Benchmark plan in your area decreased in price
- **Solution:** This is normal—it means premiums are more affordable. Your out-of-pocket may stay similar.

**Your subsidy is calculated using this formula: (2nd-lowest-cost Silver plan in your area) minus (your expected contribution based on income).** Your expected contribution is capped at 0-8.5% of your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) depending on how your income compares to Federal Poverty Level. Common reasons for differences: income changes, household size changes, benchmark plan price changes, or data matching issues. Update your income anytime at HealthCare.gov to adjust your subsidy. You'll reconcile the final amount on your tax return using Form 8962.
