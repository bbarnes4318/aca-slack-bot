# Income Qualification and Management Strategies

## Income Thresholds
### Minimum Requirements:
- **Individual:** $16,000 per year
- **Couple:** $22,000 per year
- *Note: Amounts vary by state*

## Federal Poverty Level (FPL) Verification
- **Primary Tool:** Healthcare.gov live link for state-specific minimums
- **Why use live link:** Provides most updated numbers directly from healthcare.gov
- **Alternative:** FPL chart for quick reference

### How to Use Healthcare.gov Tool
1. Navigate to healthcare.gov state-specific page
2. Select the state
3. Select household size
4. View minimum income requirements

## Handling Below-Threshold Income
### Critical Principle
**"We are NOT in the business of telling people they qualify for Medicaid and have a good day. We probe, we educate, and we ENROLL."**

## Income Probing Script
When customer provides income below threshold:

"In your state, the household income minimum requirement is $16,000 per individual / $22,000 for a couple per year to qualify. Based on what you told me, your current income is lower than that. 

Is there any additional income or other sources of income you expect this year that we haven't included, such as:
- Tips
- Bonuses
- Overtime pay
- Raises
- Side jobs/side hustle
- Any other work-related income

We want to make sure your estimate is as accurate as possible for approval."

## Additional Probing Techniques
- "Do you plan on making more money this year?"
- "Is it possible you could reach the minimum requirement?"
- Be creative: "Do you bake cookies? Do anything from home?"
- **Remember:** There's no penalty for estimating higher income, only for underestimating

## Key Education Point
"The enrollment is based on FUTURE income for 2025, not what you made last year."

## Income Entry Best Practices
### Preferred Method:
- Use YEARLY income (not hourly/monthly)
- "Quickest and easiest way"

### Critical Distinction:
"You could be self-employed making $300,000/year. All that matters is your NET income. Remember your adjusted gross income on your tax return? That's all they care about."

## Handling Deductions
**When asked about deductions:**
**ALWAYS SELECT: NO**

**Reasoning:**
- "You're not an accountant"
- Too complicated to itemize
- Risk of errors
- Use adjusted gross income ÷ 12 months
